# 10-INST-H2-003 - Cryogenic Safety Equipment

## 1. Purpose

Specification and installation of cryogenic safety equipment required for working with liquid hydrogen (LH2) at -253°C (-423°F) during AMPEL360-BWB-H2 aircraft parking and fueling operations.

## 2. Scope

- Personal protective equipment (PPE) for cryogenic operations
- Emergency safety equipment and stations
- Cryogenic first aid supplies
- Training and certification requirements

**Effectivity**: All facilities handling LH2 for AMPEL360-BWB-H2 aircraft

## 3. Applicable Documents

- **NFPA 2** - Hydrogen Technologies Code
- **29 CFR 1910.132** - OSHA PPE requirements
- **ISO 13984** - Liquid Hydrogen safety
- **CGA P-12** - Safe Handling of Cryogenic Liquids

## 4. Safety Precautions

⚠️ **WARNINGS**

- **EXTREME COLD**: LH2 at -253°C causes instant frostbite/tissue damage on contact
- **RAPID EXPANSION**: Liquid-to-gas expansion ratio 1:848 can cause pressure buildup
- **OXYGEN DEFICIENCY**: Displaces oxygen in enclosed spaces - asphyxiation hazard
- **EMBRITTLEMENT**: Many materials become brittle and fail at cryogenic temperatures
- **EYE PROTECTION MANDATORY**: Face shields required for all LH2 operations

⚠️ **CAUTIONS**

- Never touch uninsulated cryogenic surfaces or equipment
- Always wear full PPE before approaching LH2 systems
- Keep emergency safety shower and eyewash within 25 ft of work area
- Change PPE if it becomes wet or contaminated with LH2

## 5. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

### 5.1 Required PPE for LH2 Operations

**Eye and Face Protection**:
- **Face Shield**: Full-face, clear polycarbonate, minimum 2mm thick
  - Must cover entire face and extend below chin
  - Anti-fog coating recommended
  - ANSI Z87.1 certified
  - Quantity: One per person working with LH2

**Hand Protection**:
- **Cryogenic Gloves**: Loose-fitting, insulated to -253°C
  - Material: Leather outer, multi-layer insulation
  - Length: Minimum 15" (covers forearm)
  - Quick-removal design (no laces, must be able to shed rapidly)
  - ASTM F2249 certified
  - Quantity: Two pairs per person (one in use, one backup)

**Body Protection**:
- **Cryogenic Apron**: Full-length, cryogenic-rated
  - Material: Aluminized fabric or leather
  - Length: Knee to chest coverage minimum
  - Quick-release straps (emergency removal)
  - Quantity: One per person working with LH2

**Foot Protection**:
- **Safety Boots**: Steel-toe, leather, minimum 8" height
  - No cuff or folds that can trap liquid
  - Laces must be tucked or covered
  - Worn with cuffs over boot tops (prevent liquid entry)
  - ASTM F2413 certified
  - Quantity: One pair per person

**Additional Protection**:
- **Coveralls**: Cotton or flame-resistant material (not synthetic)
  - Full-body coverage
  - Worn under cryogenic apron
  - Quantity: One per person

- **Hard Hat**: Impact-resistant, cold-resistant material
  - ANSI Z89.1 certified
  - Face shield attaches to hard hat
  - Quantity: One per person

### 5.2 PPE Storage and Maintenance

**Storage Location**: Install PPE stations at each LH2 work area
- Within 10 ft of LH2 operations
- Clearly marked "CRYOGENIC PPE"
- Protected from weather and contamination
- Adequate quantity for all workers

**PPE Station Contents** (for 4 persons):
- 4 Face shields (+ 2 spares)
- 8 Pairs cryogenic gloves (2 per person)
- 4 Cryogenic aprons
- 4 Sets coveralls (various sizes)
- 4 Hard hats with face shield mounts
- PPE donning instructions placard
- PPE inspection checklist

**Maintenance**:
- **Daily**: Visual inspection before use (cracks, tears, contamination)
- **Monthly**: Detailed inspection, clean per manufacturer instructions
- **Annually**: Replace gloves and aprons (lifespan typically 1-2 years)
- **As Needed**: Replace any damaged or contaminated PPE immediately

## 6. Emergency Safety Equipment

### 6.1 Safety Shower and Eyewash Station

**Location**: Within 25 ft (10 seconds travel time) of any LH2 operation
**Type**: Combination safety shower and eyewash, plumbed to potable water supply

**Specifications**:
- **Shower**: 20 GPM flow rate, minimum 15 minutes supply
- **Eyewash**: Dual-head, 0.4 GPM per head, minimum 15 minutes supply
- **Activation**: Large paddle or pull handle (hand-free)
- **Water Temperature**: Tepid (60-100°F) via thermostatic mixing valve
- **Testing**: Weekly activation test, monthly inspection

**Installation**:
1. Install at marked location (on parking stand plan)
2. Connect to potable water supply with freeze protection
3. Install tempering/mixing valve for tepid water
4. Ensure clear path from LH2 work areas (no obstacles)
5. Install high-visibility signage ("EMERGENCY SHOWER/EYEWASH")
6. Illuminate for night operations
7. Protect from freezing in cold climates (heated enclosure or trace heating)

### 6.2 First Aid and Medical Supplies

**Cryogenic Burn Treatment Kit** (one per parking location):

Contents:
- Sterile water or saline (1 gallon) for irrigation
- Sterile gauze pads (4x4", 25 pack)
- Non-adherent dressings (Telfa, 10 pack)
- Conforming bandages (4" width, 10 rolls)
- Medical tape (1" width, 2 rolls)
- Burn gel packets (10 pack)
- Scissors (blunt-tip medical)
- Disposable gloves (nitrile, 10 pair)
- Emergency blanket (reflective, 2 each)
- First aid manual (cryogenic burns section)
- Emergency contact numbers placard

**Storage**:
- Weatherproof cabinet near safety shower
- Clearly marked "CRYOGENIC BURN FIRST AID"
- Inspect monthly, replace expired items
- Restock after any use

### 6.3 Fire Suppression Equipment

**Fire Extinguishers** (H2-Rated):
- Type: Class B/C (CO2 or dry chemical)
- Size: Minimum 20 lb capacity
- Quantity: 2 per parking location
- Location: 
  - One near H2 fueling interface (outside Zone 1)
  - One near vent outlet (25+ ft from vent)
- Mounting: On wheeled cart or fixed mount with sign
- Inspection: Monthly visual, annual service, hydrostatic test per schedule

**Fire Blanket**:
- Size: 6 ft x 8 ft minimum
- Material: Fire-resistant fabric
- Location: Near fueling area in quick-access cabinet
- Purpose: Smother small fires or wrap victim with burning clothing

### 6.4 Spill Control Equipment

**For Small LH2 Spills** (<1 gallon):
- Absorbent material: Vermiculite or perlite (5 gallon bucket)
- Plastic shovel and broom (non-sparking)
- Waste container with lid (5 gallon)
- Warning tape and cones

**Note**: LH2 spills rapidly evaporate. Primary hazard is cold vapor cloud and ignition. Containment is not practical; focus on area evacuation and ignition prevention.

## 7. Installation Procedures

### 7.1 PPE Station Installation

1. Select location: 10 ft from LH2 work area, protected from weather
2. Install cabinet or rack (wall-mounted or free-standing)
3. Stock with PPE per quantities above
4. Install instructional placard with donning procedures
5. Install inspection checklist (monthly sign-off)
6. Install emergency contact information
7. Label clearly: "CRYOGENIC PPE - FOR LH2 OPERATIONS"

### 7.2 Safety Shower Installation

1. Verify location is within 25 ft of LH2 operations
2. Install shower fixture per manufacturer instructions
3. Connect to tempered water supply (60-100°F)
4. Install eyewash heads at proper height (33-45" above floor)
5. Test water flow and temperature
6. Install drain (required for indoor installations)
7. Install signage and lighting
8. Document installation and add to test schedule

## 8. Training Requirements

### 8.1 Required Training for LH2 Operations Personnel

**Initial Training** (before working with LH2):
- Cryogenic hazards and properties of LH2
- Proper PPE selection, donning, and use
- Emergency procedures and equipment location
- Cryogenic first aid and burn treatment
- Hands-on PPE practice (donning/doffing)
- Simulated emergency scenarios

**Refresher Training**:
- Annually for all personnel
- After any incident or near-miss
- When procedures change

**Certification**:
- Written test (80% pass required)
- Practical evaluation (PPE donning, emergency response)
- Certificate valid 1 year
- Maintain training records

### 8.2 First Aid and Medical Response

**First Aid Training**:
- All LH2operations personnel should have basic first aid certification
- Specific training on cryogenic burns
- Practice with safety shower and eyewash
- Understand when to call for advanced medical help

**Emergency Medical Response**:
- Pre-arrange with local EMS for cryogenic emergency response
- Provide EMS with facility tour and hazard briefing
- Ensure EMS knows location of LH2 operations
- Post emergency contact numbers at each work area

## 9. Cryogenic First Aid Procedures

### 9.1 Cold Contact Injury (Frostbite)

**Immediate Actions**:
1. Remove person from cold source
2. Remove contaminated PPE/clothing (cut off if necessary, do not pull)
3. Do NOT rub or massage affected area
4. Warm affected area with lukewarm water (98-102°F)
5. Do NOT use hot water or direct heat
6. Cover with sterile non-adherent dressing
7. Seek medical attention immediately for anything beyond superficial injury

### 9.2 Eye Exposure

**Immediate Actions**:
1. Move person to eyewash station immediately
2. Hold eyelids open, flush with water for minimum 15 minutes
3. Do NOT allow person to rub eyes
4. After flushing, cover both eyes with sterile pads
5. Seek immediate medical attention

### 9.3 LH2 Splash on Body

**Immediate Actions**:
1. Move to safety shower immediately, activate
2. Remove contaminated clothing while under shower (cut off if necessary)
3. Continue showering for minimum 15 minutes
4. Gently remove ice if formed on skin (use water, not force)
5. Cover affected areas with clean, dry cloth
6. Seek medical attention

**DO NOT**:
- Apply ointments or creams
- Break blisters
- Use hot water
- Delay medical treatment
