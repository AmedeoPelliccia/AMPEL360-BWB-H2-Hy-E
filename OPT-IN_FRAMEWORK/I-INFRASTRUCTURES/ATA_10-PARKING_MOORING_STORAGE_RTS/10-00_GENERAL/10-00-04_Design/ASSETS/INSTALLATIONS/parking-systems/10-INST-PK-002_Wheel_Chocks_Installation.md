# 10-INST-PK-002 - Wheel Chocks Installation

## 1. Purpose

Procedures for proper installation and use of wheel chocks for AMPEL360-BWB-H2 aircraft parking operations.

## 2. Scope

- Wheel chock specifications and requirements
- Installation procedures for main and nose landing gear
- BWB-specific considerations for gear positioning
- Safety requirements and inspection criteria

**Effectivity**: All AMPEL360-BWB-H2 aircraft parking operations

## 3. Applicable Documents

- **ATA 100 Chapter 10** - Parking procedures
- **SAE AS1215** - Aircraft wheel chock specifications
- **ATA 32** - Landing Gear

## 4. Safety Precautions

⚠️ **WARNINGS**
- Never position aircraft without wheel chocks in place
- Chocks must be rated for aircraft maximum weight
- Install chocks immediately after aircraft stops
- Parking brake alone is not sufficient for parking security

## 5. Wheel Chock Specifications

### 5.1 Main Gear Chocks

**Type**: Heavy-duty rubber or urethane
**Capacity**: 100,000 lb aircraft minimum
**Dimensions**: 
- Height: 10-12" (front)
- Length: 24-30"
- Width: 12-15"
- Angle: 30-45° slope

**Material**: High-density rubber compound, resistant to oils/fuels
**Features**:
- Rope/chain handle for safe handling
- Reflective striping for visibility
- Embossed "CHOCK" marking
- Drainage holes (if rubber)

### 5.2 Nose Gear Chocks

**Type**: Medium-duty rubber or urethane
**Capacity**: 25,000 lb minimum
**Dimensions**:
- Height: 6-8" (front)
- Length: 18-24"
- Width: 10-12"
- Angle: 30-45° slope

## 6. Installation Procedure

### 6.1 Main Gear Chock Installation

**Timing**: Install immediately after aircraft comes to complete stop

**Position**: 
1. Place chock against leading edge of main gear tire
2. Position chock perpendicular to tire centerline
3. Ensure chock contacts tire firmly
4. Chock should be centered on tire width
5. Minimum 2 chocks per main gear assembly (fore and aft if available)

**BWB Main Gear Notes**:
- BWB may have wider main gear track than conventional aircraft
- Verify sufficient chock inventory for all gear positions
- Main gear is primary load-bearing for BWB weight

**Verification**:
- Chock firmly contacts tire with no gaps
- Chock is stable and will not tip over
- Chock rope/handle positioned for safe removal
- Chock is visible to pilot and ground crew

### 6.2 Nose Gear Chock Installation

**Position**:
1. Place chock against leading edge of nose gear tire
2. Position chock perpendicular to tire centerline
3. Ensure solid contact with tire
4. Single chock typically sufficient for nose gear

**BWB Nose Gear Notes**:
- BWB nose gear carries less weight than conventional aircraft (aft CG)
- Nose gear may have lower ground clearance
- Ensure chock size appropriate for tire diameter

### 6.3 Chock Removal

**Timing**: Remove only after receiving clearance and verifying:
- Pilot ready to taxi
- Tow tractor connected (if towing)
- All ground personnel and equipment clear
- Parking brake released or tow bar engaged

**Procedure**:
1. Remove nose gear chock first
2. Stand clear of aircraft movement path
3. Remove main gear chocks
4. Use rope/handle - do not reach under tire
5. Signal "CHOCKS AWAY" to pilot/tow operator
6. Store chocks immediately after removal

## 7. BWB Considerations

### 7.1 Gear Configuration

**Main Gear**:
- BWB may have distributed main gear (multiple bogies)
- Chock each main gear truck
- Consider gear placement for wide wing structure
- Primary parking restraint due to aft CG

**Nose Gear**:
- Lower load than conventional aircraft
- May be more lightly loaded (tail-heavy BWB)
- Still requires chocking for complete restraint
- Smaller chock may be sufficient

### 7.2 Ground Clearance

- BWB has lower overall ground clearance
- Ensure chocks do not interfere with lower fuselage/wing
- Use appropriate chock height for tire diameter
- Clear path for chock installation and removal

## 8. H2 Considerations

- Chocks must not obstruct H2 fueling operations
- Position chocks to allow emergency egress paths
- Use non-sparking chock material near H2 areas (rubber preferred)
- Ensure chocks are electrically non-conductive

## 9. Inspection and Maintenance

### 9.1 Pre-Use Inspection

Before each use, inspect chocks for:
- Cracks, tears, or deterioration
- Excessive wear on contact surface
- Damaged rope/handle
- Missing reflective markings
- Oil/fuel contamination (clean if necessary)

### 9.2 Replacement Criteria

Replace chocks if:
- Material cracked or torn
- Wear depth >1/4" on contact surface
- Rope/handle damaged or missing
- Chock deformed or unstable
- Does not maintain position under tire

### 9.3 Cleaning and Storage

- Clean chocks after each use with water and mild detergent
- Remove oil/fuel contamination immediately
- Store in covered area protected from UV and weather
- Stack neatly to prevent deformation
- Maintain inventory of sufficient chocks for fleet

## 10. Quality Assurance

- Chocks must meet SAE AS1215 or equivalent
- Monthly inspection of chock inventory
- Documentation of chock condition and replacement
- Training for all ground personnel on proper chock installation
