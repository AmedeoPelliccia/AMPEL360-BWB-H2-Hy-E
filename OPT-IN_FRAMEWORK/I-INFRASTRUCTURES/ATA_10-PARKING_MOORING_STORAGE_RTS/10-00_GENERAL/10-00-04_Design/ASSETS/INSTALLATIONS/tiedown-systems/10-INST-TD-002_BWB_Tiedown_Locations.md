# 10-INST-TD-002 - BWB Tiedown Locations

## 1. Purpose

This document specifies the approved tiedown locations for the AMPEL360-BWB-H2 aircraft, accounting for the unique Blended Wing Body configuration and hydrogen fuel system considerations.

## 2. Scope

This document covers:
- Primary and secondary tiedown point locations on BWB airframe
- Center of gravity considerations for load distribution
- Minimum tiedown configuration requirements
- Prohibited areas and installation restrictions

**Effectivity**: All AMPEL360-BWB-H2 aircraft configurations

## 4. Safety Precautions

⚠️ **WARNINGS**

- **MINIMUM TIEDOWNS**: Never operate with fewer than 6 tiedown points secured for BWB configuration
- **CENTER OF GRAVITY**: Improper tiedown location selection may cause aircraft instability in high winds
- **LOAD DISTRIBUTION**: Asymmetric tiedown may induce structural loads exceeding design limits
- **H2 SYSTEM**: Maintain minimum clearance from LH2 tank vents and pressure relief devices

⚠️ **CAUTIONS**

- Verify aircraft weight and CG before selecting tiedown configuration
- Do not use tiedown points showing signs of damage or wear
- Account for fuel load (especially LH2) when determining load distribution

### 7.3 Location Verification Procedure

#### 7.3.1 Using Aircraft Datum

1. Identify aircraft datum reference (forward pressure bulkhead, Station 0.0)
2. Measure aft from datum to each station using measuring tape
3. Mark stations with chalk line or temporary marker
4. Verify measurements with independent inspector

#### 7.3.2 Using Buttock Lines

1. Establish aircraft centerline using plumb bob from reference points
2. Measure laterally from centerline to buttock lines
3. Account for aircraft attitude (level aircraft first if possible)
4. Mark buttock lines with chalk line

#### 7.3.3 Using Waterlines

1. Level aircraft using jacks or leveling equipment (if available)
2. Measure vertically from ground reference or aircraft datum
3. Account for ground slope and tire deflection
4. Verify waterline measurements

**DO NOT attach tiedowns to or near**:

- Control surfaces (ailerons, elevators, rudders)
- Control surface hinges and actuators

- Minimum clearance: 12 inches from any moving control surface

#### 7.4.3 Access Doors and Panels

**Maintain clearance for maintenance access**:

- Personnel doors: 3 ft clearance minimum
- Service panels: 2 ft clearance minimum
- Emergency exits: 5 ft clearance minimum

## 8. H2/BWB Considerations

### 8.1 BWB Configuration Impact

**Wide Wingspan**: The BWB configuration has a wingspan of approximately XXX ft, requiring:
- Additional outboard tiedown points (4-6 total on wings)
- Greater spacing between tiedown anchors on ground
- Consideration of wing twist under asymmetric wind loading

**Low Ground Clearance**: The BWB has lower ground clearance than conventional aircraft:
- Some tiedown points may be difficult to access
- Use of low-profile tiedown equipment may be required
- Wing tips are closer to ground, requiring careful equipment positioning

**Integrated Structure**: Wing and body are integrated in BWB:
- Load paths are different from conventional aircraft
- Some tiedown points are on blended wing-body transition
- Structural analysis required for any additional tiedown locations

### 8.2 H2 System Considerations

**Cryogenic Safety**: LH2 operates at -253°C (-423°F):
- Tiedown equipment must not contact cryogenic lines or components
- Personnel must wear appropriate PPE when working near LH2 system
- Emergency procedures must be in place for LH2 leaks

**Bonding and Grounding**: H2 safety requires:
- Each tiedown point must have verified electrical bonding to aircraft
- Bonding resistance < 0.003 ohms
- Bonding verification before any H2 fueling operations

**Venting During Parking**: LH2 may vent during parking:
- Maintain clear zone around vent outlets (15 ft radius minimum)
- Post warning signs for H2 venting operations
- Monitor H2 concentration if enclosed hangar operations
