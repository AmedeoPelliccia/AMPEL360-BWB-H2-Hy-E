# 10-INST-MR-004 - Storm Mooring Setup

## 1. Purpose

Emergency procedures for securing AMPEL360-BWB-H2 aircraft during severe weather events with winds exceeding 60 knots.

## 2. Scope

- Storm preparation procedures and timeline
- Maximum mooring configuration installation
- Continuous monitoring requirements
- Emergency response procedures

**Effectivity**: All AMPEL360-BWB-H2 aircraft during severe weather warnings

## 3. Applicable Documents

- **10-INST-MR-003** - BWB Mooring Configuration
- **10-INST-TD-002** - BWB Tiedown Locations
- **National Weather Service** - Severe weather alerts
- **Local Airport Authority** - Emergency procedures

## 4. Safety Precautions

⚠️ **WARNINGS**
- Storm mooring requires minimum 4-6 hours preparation time
- Personnel must not work on aircraft when winds exceed 40 knots
- Aircraft must be evacuated of all fuel and fluids if possible
- Hangar storage preferred if available for winds >80 knots

⚠️ **CAUTIONS**
- Secure all loose equipment and panels before storm
- Remove external equipment (pitot covers, chocks, etc.)
- Document aircraft configuration for post-storm inspection

## 5. Storm Warning Procedures

### 5.1 Alert Levels

**Level 1 - Storm Watch** (>48 hours out)
- Monitor weather forecasts
- Review storm mooring procedures with crew
- Inspect mooring equipment and verify availability
- Identify sheltered locations or hangar space

**Level 2 - Storm Warning** (24-48 hours out)
- Initiate storm preparation procedures
- Position aircraft in most sheltered location available
- Begin aircraft preparation (close panels, remove equipment)
- Assemble mooring equipment and crew

**Level 3 - Storm Imminent** (6-24 hours out)
- Execute maximum mooring configuration
- Complete aircraft securing operations
- Evacuate non-essential personnel
- Establish continuous monitoring protocol

**Level 4 - Storm in Progress** (winds >60 knots)
- All personnel evacuated to safe location
- Remote monitoring only (video cameras, wind sensors)
- Emergency response team on standby
- No access to aircraft until winds <40 knots

### 5.2 Decision Criteria

**Hangar Storage** (preferred if available):
- Forecast winds >80 knots
- Hurricane or tornado warning
- Aircraft can be moved safely (winds currently <30 knots)

**Storm Mooring** (if hangar not available):
- Forecast winds 60-80 knots
- Adequate mooring equipment and ground anchors available
- Sufficient time for installation (minimum 4 hours)

**Evacuation** (if storm too severe):
- Forecast winds >100 knots
- Inadequate mooring facilities
- Consider ferrying aircraft to safer location if airworthy and safe to fly

## 6. Storm Mooring Configuration

### 6.1 Maximum Mooring Setup

**Total Mooring Points**: 10-12 points

**Primary Attachment Points** (required):
1. Forward Port (FP)
2. Forward Starboard (FS)
3. Wing Port Outboard (WPO)
4. Wing Starboard Outboard (WSO)
5. Wing Port Inboard (WPI)
6. Wing Starboard Inboard (WSI)
7. Aft Port (AP)
8. Aft Starboard (AS)

**Secondary Attachment Points** (if available):
9. Nose tiedown (if structurally approved)
10. Tail tiedown (if available)
11. Additional wing points (if available)
12. Landing gear restraints (if approved)

### 6.2 Mooring Equipment Requirements

| Equipment | Quantity | Specification |
|-----------|----------|---------------|
| Wire Rope | 12 x 100 ft | 1" diameter, 32,000 lb capacity |
| Chain | 12 x 20 ft | Grade 80, 5/8" diameter |
| Shackles | 24 | 12,000 lb capacity, screw-pin type |
| Load Equalizers | 4 | 3-leg, 40,000 lb capacity |
| Ground Anchors | 8-10 | 20,000 lb capacity each |
| Turnbuckles | 12 | 10,000 lb capacity, for tensioning |

### 6.3 Installation Sequence

**Preparation Phase** (6+ hours before storm):

1. **Position aircraft**
   - Move to most sheltered location
   - Orient nose into prevailing wind (if known)
   - Level aircraft using jacks (if safe to do so)

2. **Aircraft preparation**
   - Close and secure all doors and panels
   - Remove pitot covers and external equipment
   - Deflate tires to 50% pressure (prevents movement)
   - Set parking brake and install gear pins
   - Drain or minimize fuel load if possible
   - Cover engine inlets and exhausts

3. **Ground anchor preparation**
   - Inspect all ground anchors
   - Load test anchors to 125% of design load
   - Position additional portable anchors as needed
   - Document anchor locations

**Mooring Installation Phase** (4-6 hours before storm):

1. **Install primary mooring bridles**
   - Forward points first (FP, FS)
   - Wing points next (WPO, WSO, WPI, WSI)
   - Aft points last (AP, AS)
   - Connect to load equalizers

2. **Install secondary restraints**
   - Nose and tail restraints (if used)
   - Landing gear restraints (if approved)
   - Additional wing restraints

3. **Connect to ground anchors**
   - Distribute load among multiple anchors
   - Use turnbuckles to tension mooring lines
   - Achieve 10-15% of design load pre-tension
   - Verify symmetric load distribution

4. **Final tensioning**
   - Adjust all turnbuckles for equal tension
   - Remove all slack from mooring lines
   - Check aircraft attitude (wings level, nose down slightly)
   - Verify no interference or chafing

**Final Checks** (1-2 hours before storm):

1. Walk-around inspection of all mooring connections
2. Verify all hardware is secure and locked
3. Check for any loose items around aircraft
4. Install warning tape and signs around aircraft
5. Photograph configuration for documentation
6. Brief monitoring team on inspection points

## 7. Continuous Monitoring Protocol

### 7.1 Monitoring Equipment

- **Wind sensors**: Anemometer at aircraft location, data logger
- **Video cameras**: Multiple angles, remote viewing capability
- **Communication**: Radio or phone with monitoring team
- **Emergency lights**: For night operations

### 7.2 Inspection Schedule

**Before storm** (winds <60 knots):
- Inspection every 2 hours
- Adjust mooring tension as needed
- Re-torque any loose hardware

**During storm** (winds >60 knots):
- Remote monitoring only (video, sensors)
- No personnel access to aircraft
- Document wind speeds and aircraft behavior
- Emergency response team on standby

**After storm** (winds <40 knots):
- Immediate inspection for any damage or movement
- Check all mooring connections
- Document any failures or issues
- Begin controlled release of mooring

### 7.3 Emergency Response

**If mooring failure occurs**:
- Evacuate all personnel immediately
- Do not attempt to re-secure aircraft in high winds
- Monitor aircraft movement remotely
- Coordinate with airport emergency services
- Plan for post-storm recovery

## 8. Storm Mooring Release

### 8.1 Release Criteria

- Wind speed sustained <40 knots for 1 hour minimum
- No severe weather forecast for next 24 hours
- Daylight hours (if possible)
- Qualified personnel available

### 8.2 Release Procedure

1. **Inspection**
   - Complete walk-around inspection
   - Document any damage or issues
   - Check all mooring hardware condition
   - Photograph current configuration

2. **Gradual release**
   - Release secondary mooring first (nose, tail, gear)
   - Release wing mooring gradually
   - Maintain forward and aft mooring until last
   - Monitor aircraft movement during release

3. **Post-release inspection**
   - Inspect all aircraft tiedown points for damage
   - Check aircraft attitude and gear
   - Inspect for any storm damage
   - Document aircraft condition

4. **Equipment recovery**
   - Remove and inspect all mooring equipment
   - Clean and store properly
   - Document any damaged equipment for replacement
   - Update equipment inventory

## 9. H2/BWB Considerations

### 9.1 BWB Storm Loads

- BWB experiences 50% higher lateral wind loads in storm
- Large wing surface area acts as sail in crosswinds
- Requires maximum mooring configuration even for moderate storms
- Consider hangar storage for winds >70 knots

### 9.2 H2 Safety During Storm

- Ensure all H2 systems are secured and inert if possible
- Verify H2 detection systems are operational
- Maintain emergency response capability
- Brief emergency responders on H2 hazards

## 10. Quality Assurance

- Storm mooring checklist must be completed for each installation
- Photographic documentation required
- Wind speed data logging during storm
- Post-storm damage inspection and report
