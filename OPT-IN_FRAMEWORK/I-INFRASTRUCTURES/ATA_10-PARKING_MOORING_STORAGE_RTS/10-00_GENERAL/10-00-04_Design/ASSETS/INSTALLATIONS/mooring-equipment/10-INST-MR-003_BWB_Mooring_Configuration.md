# 10-INST-MR-003 - BWB Mooring Configuration

## 1. Purpose

Specific mooring configurations and procedures for the AMPEL360-BWB-H2 Blended Wing Body aircraft, accounting for unique aerodynamic surface area and center of gravity.

## 2. Scope

- BWB-specific mooring patterns and load distribution
- Wind load considerations for large lifting surface
- Mooring bridle configuration and attachment points
- Special considerations for integrated wing-body design

**Effectivity**: AMPEL360-BWB-H2 aircraft only

## 3. Applicable Documents

- **10-INST-TD-002** - BWB Tiedown Locations
- **10-INST-MR-001** - Mooring Mast Installation
- **10-INST-TD-004** - Tiedown Load Requirements
- **CS-25** - Large aeroplane certification

## 4. Safety Precautions

⚠️ **WARNINGS**
- BWB configuration experiences 50% higher lateral wind loads than conventional aircraft
- Never use conventional aircraft mooring patterns on BWB aircraft
- Center of gravity is farther aft than conventional aircraft - adjust mooring accordingly
- Large wing surface area requires additional mooring points in high winds

### 5.3 Storm Configuration (Winds 60-80 knots)

**Mooring Pattern**: 10-point maximum mooring + nose/tail restraints

**All Available Attachment Points Including**:
- All primary and secondary tiedown points
- Nose tiedown (if structurally approved)
- Tail tiedown (if available)

**Load Distribution**:
- Distributed based on wind direction
- Windward side: 60% of load
- Leeward side: 40% of load

**Critical Requirements**:
- Continuous wind monitoring required
- Personnel on-site or on-call
- Inspection every 2 hours
- Aircraft must be empty or minimal fuel

## 6. BWB Aerodynamic Considerations

### 6.1 Wind Surface Area

**BWB Large Lifting Surface**:
- Frontal area (head/tail wind): XXX ft²
- Lateral area (beam wind): XXX ft² (significantly larger than conventional)
- Plan area (vertical wind): XXX ft²

**Implication**: Lateral wind loads are approximately 50% higher than equivalent-weight conventional aircraft.

### 6.2 Center of Gravity Effects

**BWB CG Location**: Approximately Station XXX (farther aft than conventional)

**Effects on Mooring**:
- Greater load on aft mooring points (45% vs 35% for conventional)
- Tail-down attitude in high winds if not properly secured
- Potential for nose lift if aft mooring insufficient

**Mitigation**:
- Ensure aft mooring points are primary load path
- Monitor aircraft attitude during high winds
- Add forward restraints if nose lift observed

### 6.3 Wing Flexibility

**BWB Wing Structure**:
- Large span with significant flexibility
- Wing deflection under asymmetric wind loads
- Potential for wing twist in beam winds

**Mitigation**:
- Distribute mooring loads across multiple wing attachment points
- Use both inboard and outboard wing mooring points in high winds
- Monitor wing deflection during operations

## 7. Mooring Bridle Configuration

### 7.1 Bridle Design for BWB

**Y-Bridle Configuration**:
- Primary bridle from mooring mast to aircraft CG area
- Secondary bridles to wing attachment points
- Tertiary bridles to nose and tail (high wind only)

**Bridle Materials**:
- Main bridle: 1" wire rope, 32,000 lb capacity
- Secondary bridles: 3/4" wire rope, 18,000 lb capacity
- Connections: Grade 80 chain and shackles

**Bridle Angles**:
- Main bridle to mast: 30-45° from horizontal
- Wing bridles: 20-30° from horizontal
- Avoid angles >60° (excessive vertical loads)

### 7.2 Load Equalizers

**Use load equalizers** to distribute loads among multiple attachment points:
- 3-leg equalizer for symmetric distribution
- Adjustable for uneven loads
- Rated for maximum expected load + 50% safety factor

## 8. Installation Procedure

### 8.1 Pre-Mooring Checks

1. Verify aircraft weight and CG (ref ATA 02 Weight & Balance)
2. Inspect all mooring attachment points for damage
3. Check weather forecast for next 24 hours
4. Select appropriate mooring configuration
5. Verify mooring equipment condition and capacity

### 8.2 Mooring Installation

1. **Position aircraft** at mooring location
   - Align aircraft CG with mooring mast (if used)
   - Set parking brake and install wheel chocks
   - Ensure wings level (use jacks if necessary)

2. **Install forward mooring bridles**
   - Connect to forward tiedown points (FP, FS)
   - Adjust length to ensure equal tension
   - Verify proper hardware installation

3. **Install wing mooring bridles**
   - Connect to wing tiedown points (WPO, WSO)
   - For high wind: add inboard wing points (WPI, WSI)
   - Ensure bridles clear wing surfaces and systems

4. **Install aft mooring bridles**
   - Connect to aft tiedown points (AP, AS)
   - Verify load distribution per configuration requirements
   - Ensure greatest load capacity at aft points

5. **Connect to mooring mast or ground anchors**
   - Attach all bridles to load equalizer or mast
   - Adjust tension to remove slack without preloading
   - Verify all connections secure

6. **Final checks**
   - All connections tight and locked
   - Bridle angles within limits
   - No chafing or interference
   - Aircraft attitude level
   - Document configuration

### 8.3 Post-Mooring Inspection

- Inspect mooring after 1 hour of installation
- Re-check tension and connections
- Look for any movement or settling
- Adjust as necessary

## 9. H2/BWB Considerations

### 9.1 H2 System Clearances

- Maintain 15 ft clearance from H2 vent outlets
- Mooring bridles must not contact LH2 system components
- Verify bonding of all mooring equipment to aircraft ground

### 9.2 BWB Access

- Ensure mooring configuration does not block access doors
- Maintain clear path for emergency H2 response
- Position ground anchors to not interfere with GSE access

## 10. Quality Assurance

- Mooring configuration must be approved by qualified person
- Inspection required every 12 hours during mooring operations
- Wind speed monitoring with alert at 35 knots
- Documentation of mooring configuration and inspections
