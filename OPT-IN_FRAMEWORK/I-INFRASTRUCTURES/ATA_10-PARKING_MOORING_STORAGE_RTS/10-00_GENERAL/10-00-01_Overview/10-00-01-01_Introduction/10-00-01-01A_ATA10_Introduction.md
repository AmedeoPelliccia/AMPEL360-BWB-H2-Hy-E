# 10-00-01-01A - ATA 10 Introduction

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a comprehensive introduction to ATA Chapter 10 (Parking, Mooring, Storage and Return to Service) as applied to the AMPEL360-BWB-H2 hydrogen-powered aircraft with Blended Wing Body configuration.

## 2. Scope

This document establishes the foundational understanding of parking, mooring, storage, and return-to-service operations for the AMPEL360-BWB-H2 aircraft, including specific considerations for:

- Hydrogen fuel system management during ground operations
- BWB-specific geometric and access requirements
- Safety protocols for H2/LH2 systems in parking and storage conditions
- Regulatory compliance framework

## 4. Description

### 4.1 Overview

ATA Chapter 10 addresses the essential ground operations for aircraft when not in active flight service. For the AMPEL360-BWB-H2, these operations are significantly influenced by:

1. **Hydrogen Fuel System**: Unique safety and operational requirements for liquid hydrogen storage and management
2. **BWB Configuration**: Non-traditional aircraft geometry affecting parking space, ground handling, and access
3. **Environmental Systems**: Cryogenic system monitoring and boil-off management during extended parking

### 4.2 H2/LH2 Considerations

The AMPEL360-BWB-H2 utilizes liquid hydrogen (LH2) as its primary fuel source, which introduces critical operational considerations:

- **Cryogenic Management**: LH2 stored at -253°C requires continuous monitoring during parking
- **Boil-off Gas Management**: Natural hydrogen evaporation must be safely vented or captured
- **Safety Zones**: Extended clearance areas around fuel system components
- **Fire Prevention**: Enhanced safety protocols for hydrogen operations
- **Ground Support Integration**: Specialized equipment for H2 servicing and monitoring

### 4.3 BWB Configuration Considerations

The Blended Wing Body design presents unique characteristics for ground operations:

- **Wider Footprint**: Increased wingspan and body width compared to conventional aircraft
- **Lower Ground Clearance**: Reduced vertical clearance in certain areas
- **Access Points**: Non-conventional door and service panel locations
- **Center of Gravity**: Different weight distribution affecting tiedown requirements
- **Ground Equipment Compatibility**: Specialized GSE for BWB geometry

## 6. Safety Considerations

### 6.1 Hydrogen Safety

- Hydrogen detection systems must be active during all ground operations
- Personnel must maintain minimum safe distances from LH2 system components
- Fire suppression equipment must be H2-compatible and readily available
- Emergency procedures must be briefed to all ground personnel

### 6.2 BWB-Specific Safety

- Ground personnel awareness of low-clearance areas
- Proper positioning of ground support equipment to avoid contact
- Enhanced visual markers for BWB extremities
- Special considerations for towing and pushback operations

### 6.3 Environmental Considerations

- Cryogenic exposure hazards for personnel and equipment
- Hydrogen venting procedures and atmospheric considerations
- Ice formation on LH2 system components
- Temperature monitoring of critical areas
