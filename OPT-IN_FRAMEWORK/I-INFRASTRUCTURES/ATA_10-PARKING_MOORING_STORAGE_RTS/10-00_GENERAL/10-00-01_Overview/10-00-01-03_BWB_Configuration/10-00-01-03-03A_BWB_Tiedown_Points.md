# 10-00-01-03-03A - BWB Tiedown Points

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the tiedown and mooring point specifications for the AMPEL360-BWB-H2 Blended Wing Body aircraft, ensuring secure aircraft restraint during parking, mooring, and storage operations.

## 2. Scope

This document covers:

- Tiedown point locations and load ratings
- Mooring procedures for various weather conditions
- Equipment specifications for BWB tiedown operations
- Special considerations for long-term storage

## 4. Description

### 4.1 Overview

The BWB configuration requires specialized tiedown provisions due to:

- **Distributed Load Structure**: Load paths different from conventional aircraft
- **Wide Footprint**: Tiedown points must accommodate BWB geometry
- **Integrated Design**: No separate wing attachment points as in conventional aircraft
- **Center of Gravity**: Unique CG location affects tiedown strategy

### 4.2 H2/LH2Considerations

Tiedown operations must consider:

- LH2 tank structural integration within BWB
- Avoid loading on cryogenic system components
- Tiedown equipment must not interfere with H2 venting
- Safety zones remain clear during tiedown operations

### 4.3 BWB Configuration Considerations

#### 4.3.1 Tiedown Point Locations

The AMPEL360-BWB-H2 is equipped with **9 primary tiedown points**:

**Forward Tiedown Points (3 points):**
- **FWD-LEFT (TDP-01)**: Located 12m from nose, 8m left of centerline
  - Load rating: 45 kN (10,000 lbf) ultimate
  - Direction: Forward and outboard (45° to centerline)
  - Access: Ground level, requires 3m access clearance

- **FWD-CENTER (TDP-02)**: Located 10m from nose, on centerline
  - Load rating: 50 kN (11,250 lbf) ultimate
  - Direction: Forward (aligned with centerline)
  - Access: Ground level, requires 3m access clearance

- **FWD-RIGHT (TDP-03)**: Located 12m from nose, 8m right of centerline
  - Load rating: 45 kN (10,000 lbf) ultimate
  - Direction: Forward and outboard (45° to centerline)
  - Access: Ground level, requires 3m access clearance

**Mid-Body Tiedown Points (4 points):**
- **MID-LEFT-INNER (TDP-04)**: Located 28m from nose, 15m left of centerline
  - Load rating: 55 kN (12,375 lbf) ultimate
  - Direction: Lateral outboard
  - Access: Via platform, 2.5m height

- **MID-LEFT-OUTER (TDP-05)**: Located 28m from nose, 30m left of centerline
  - Load rating: 40 kN (9,000 lbf) ultimate
  - Direction: Lateral outboard
  - Access: Via platform, 2.0m height

- **MID-RIGHT-INNER (TDP-06)**: Located 28m from nose, 15m right of centerline
  - Load rating: 55 kN (12,375 lbf) ultimate
  - Direction: Lateral outboard
  - Access: Via platform, 2.5m height

- **MID-RIGHT-OUTER (TDP-07)**: Located 28m from nose, 30m right of centerline
  - Load rating: 40 kN (9,000 lbf) ultimate
  - Direction: Lateral outboard
  - Access: Via platform, 2.0m height

**Aft Tiedown Points (2 points):**
- **AFT-LEFT (TDP-08)**: Located 50m from nose, 10m left of centerline
  - Load rating: 45 kN (10,000 lbf) ultimate
  - Direction: Aft and outboard (30° to centerline)
  - Access: Ground level, requires 3m access clearance

- **AFT-RIGHT (TDP-09)**: Located 50m from nose, 10m right of centerline
  - Load rating: 45 kN (10,000 lbf) ultimate
  - Direction: Aft and outboard (30° to centerline)
  - Access: Ground level, requires 3m access clearance

#### 4.3.2 Tiedown Equipment

**Tiedown Chains/Ropes:**
- Material: Galvanized steel chain or synthetic rope (approved types only)
- Minimum breaking strength: 2× rated tiedown point load
- Length: Variable, 5-15 meters typical
- Inspection: Before each use, per maintenance manual

**Ground Anchors:**
- Type: Permanent concrete anchors or mobile anchors (approved)
- Load rating: Minimum 1.5× tiedown point load
- Spacing: Positioned to achieve proper tiedown angles
- Installation: Per airport standards and AMPEL360 requirements

**Hardware:**
- Shackles: Forged steel, rated per tiedown load
- Turnbuckles: For tension adjustment, safety-wired
- Load indicators: Recommended for critical moorings
- Protective sleeves: To prevent aircraft skin damage

#### 4.3.3 Tiedown Configurations

**Normal Parking (Winds < 40 knots):**
- Minimum 3 tiedown points required
- Configuration: FWD-CENTER, AFT-LEFT, AFT-RIGHT
- Approximate load per tiedown: 15 kN (3,375 lbf)

**High Wind Parking (Winds 40-60 knots):**
- Minimum 6 tiedown points required
- Configuration: All forward (3) + all aft (2) + MID-LEFT-INNER or MID-RIGHT-INNER (1)
- Approximate load per tiedown: 25 kN (5,625 lbf)

**Severe Weather/Long-Term Storage (Winds > 60 knots potential):**
- All 9 tiedown points required
- Additional tail weight or ballast recommended
- Covers installed to reduce wind loading
- Inspection interval: Daily

#### 4.3.4 Tiedown Angles and Geometry

**Optimal Tiedown Angles:**
- Forward tiedown: 30-45° forward of lateral
- Lateral tiedown: 85-95° to aircraft centerline (nearly perpendicular)
- Aft tiedown: 30-45° aft of lateral

**Ground Anchor Positioning:**
- Forward anchors: 15-20m ahead of tiedown points
- Lateral anchors: 10-15m outboard of tiedown points
- Aft anchors: 15-20m behind tiedown points
- Anchor spacing accounts for BWB-wide footprint

## 6. Safety Considerations

### 6.1 Tiedown Installation Procedures

**Pre-Mooring:**
1. Review weather forecast for parking period
2. Determine tiedown configuration required
3. Inspect all tiedown equipment
4. Verify ground anchor availability and condition
5. Position aircraft per parking plan
6. Set parking brake, install chocks

**Mooring Installation:**
1. Attach tiedown equipment to aircraft points in sequence:
   - Forward points first
   - Mid-body points second (if required)
   - Aft points last
2. Route tiedown lines to ground anchors
3. Adjust tension to remove slack (not over-tensioned)
4. Install safety clips/pins on all connections
5. Verify proper angles achieved
6. Document tiedown configuration

**Post-Mooring:**
1. Final inspection of all connections
2. Verify clearances to ground equipment
3. Post warning signs regarding moored aircraft
4. Log tiedown installation in aircraft records

### 6.2 Mooring Inspection

**Daily Inspection (During Mooring):**
- Visual check of all tiedown connections
- Check for loose hardware or fraying
- Verify proper tension maintained
- Check for aircraft movement or shifting
- Document inspection

**Post-Weather Event Inspection:**
- Immediate inspection after winds > 50 knots
- Check for damaged or overstressed equipment
- Verify aircraft position unchanged
- Replace any questionable equipment
- Re-tension as necessary

### 6.3 Tiedown Removal

**Pre-Removal:**
1. Verify aircraft ready for flight or movement
2. Inspect tiedown equipment one final time
3. Remove in reverse order of installation:
   - Aft points first
   - Mid-body points second (if installed)
   - Forward points last
4. Stow equipment properly
5. Inspect tiedown points for damage
6. Log removal in aircraft records

### 6.4 Special Considerations

**LH2 System:**
- Ensure tiedown operations do not interfere with H2 venting
- Maintain H2 safety zones during mooring operations
- Brief personnel on H2 hazards before tiedown work

**BWB Structure:**
- Do not attach tiedown equipment to non-approved points
- Avoid loading on control surfaces or access panels
- Use protective sleeves at all contact points
- Report any structural anomalies to maintenance

**Environmental:**
- Ice/snow accumulation on tiedown equipment reduces strength
- UV exposure degrades synthetic ropes over time
- Salt air accelerates corrosion of metal components
- Temperature extremes affect equipment properties
