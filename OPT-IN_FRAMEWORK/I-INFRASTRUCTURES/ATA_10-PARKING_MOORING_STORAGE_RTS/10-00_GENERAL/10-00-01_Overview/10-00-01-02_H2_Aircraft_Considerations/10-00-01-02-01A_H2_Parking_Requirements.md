# 10-00-01-02-01A - H2 Parking Requirements

## 4. Description

### 4.1 Overview

Hydrogen-powered aircraft require enhanced parking specifications due to the unique properties of hydrogen fuel:

- **High Flammability**: Wide flammability range (4-75% in air)
- **Low Ignition Energy**: Easy ignition compared to conventional fuels
- **Rapid Dispersion**: Hydrogen is lighter than air and disperses quickly
- **Cryogenic Hazards**: LH2 stored at -253°C presents cold burn risks
- **Boil-off Management**: Natural evaporation requires continuous or periodic venting

### 4.2 H2/LH2Considerations

#### 4.2.1 Hydrogen Safety Zones

Three concentric safety zones are established around H2 fuel system components:

**Zone 1 - Exclusion Zone (0-3 meters)**
- No ignition sources permitted
- Personnel access restricted to trained H2 handlers
- Continuous H2 detection required
- Fire suppression equipment staged

**Zone 2 - Restricted Zone (3-8 meters)**
- Limited ignition sources with permits
- All personnel must have H2 safety awareness training
- Periodic H2 monitoring required
- Emergency equipment accessible

**Zone 3 - Caution Zone (8-15 meters)**
- Standard airport safety protocols apply
- H2 safety signage visible
- Emergency response routes maintained clear
- Normal ground operations permitted with awareness

#### 4.2.2 Ventilation Requirements

**Open-Air Parking:**
- Minimum air circulation: Natural wind patterns adequate
- Obstruction clearance: 5 meters minimum from structures
- Overhead clearance: 10 meters minimum for hydrogen dispersion
- Wind direction monitoring recommended for prolonged parking

**Hangar Parking:**
- Forced ventilation: Minimum 6 air changes per hour
- Hydrogen detection systems: Continuous monitoring at ceiling level
- Ventilation failure alarms: Automatic notification system
- Emergency ventilation: Backup system capability required
- Ceiling height: Minimum 15 meters recommended for H2 accumulation prevention

### 4.3 BWB Configuration Considerations

#### 4.3.1 Parking Geometry for H2 Operations

The BWB configuration affects H2 safety zone implementation:

- **Wider Footprint**: Safety zones extend further laterally
- **H2 Tank Locations**: BWB-integrated tanks require zone mapping
- **Vent Locations**: BWB geometry influences vent discharge patterns
- **Access Points**: BWB design affects safety zone ingress/egress

#### 4.3.2 Ground Clearance and H2 Equipment

- Minimum ground clearance for LH2 system components: 0.5 meters
- H2 detection sensor placement: Account for BWB geometry
- Safety zone marking: Visible from all approaches to aircraft

## 6. Safety Considerations

### 6.1 Fire Prevention

- All electrical equipment within Zone 1 must be intrinsically safe
- Static discharge grounding required before personnel approach
- Hot work permits prohibited within Zone 2
- Continuous fire watch during maintenance operations

### 6.2 Personnel Safety

- PPE requirements for Zone 1 access:
  - Cryogenic gloves and face protection
  - Anti-static footwear
  - H2 detection badge (optional but recommended)
- Maximum occupancy limits in Zone 1: 4 personnel
- Communication systems required: Radio or direct line of sight

### 6.3 Emergency Response

- Emergency shutdown procedures accessible at parking location
- Fire response equipment staged per NFPA 2 requirements
- Evacuation routes clearly marked and maintained clear
- Emergency contact information posted at parking position

### 6.4 Environmental Monitoring

- Wind speed and direction monitoring during parking operations
- Temperature monitoring of LH2 system components
- Ambient temperature considerations for boil-off rates
- Humidity monitoring for ice formation potential
