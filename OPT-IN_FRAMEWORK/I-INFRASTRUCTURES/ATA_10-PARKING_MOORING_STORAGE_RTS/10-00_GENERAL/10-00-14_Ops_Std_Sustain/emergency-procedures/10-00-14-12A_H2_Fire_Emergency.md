## ⚠️ DANGER — HYDROGEN FIRE

**IMMEDIATE ACTIONS:**
1. **EVACUATE** 50m minimum (thermal radiation hazard)
2. **ALERT** ARFF and emergency services IMMEDIATELY
3. **DO NOT** attempt to extinguish H₂ fire
4. **COOL** surrounding areas with water spray from safe distance
5. **EVACUATE** downwind areas (toxic combustion products possible)

**H₂ FLAME IS NEARLY INVISIBLE** — Use thermal imaging to detect

---

## 1. H₂ Fire Characteristics

### 1.1 Unique Properties

**H₂ fires are unique:**
- **Nearly invisible flame** in daylight (faint blue in darkness)
- **High radiant heat** (2045°C / 3713°F flame temperature)
- **Burns vertically** (H₂ rises rapidly)
- **Clean combustion** (produces only water vapor)
- **Cannot be extinguished** with conventional methods

**Detection:**
- **Thermal imaging camera** (primary method)
- **Heat sensation** at distance
- **Distortion** of air (heat shimmer)
- **Water vapor** (combustion product)
- **Sound** (roar or whoosh)

### 1.2 Hazards

| Hazard | Description | Protection |
|--------|-------------|------------|
| **Thermal Radiation** | Intense radiant heat can cause burns at distance | Maintain 50m minimum, fire-resistant PPE |
| **Invisible Flame** | Cannot see flame approach | Thermal imaging, cautious approach |
| **Hot Surfaces** | Aircraft surfaces become extremely hot | Cooling with water spray |
| **Secondary Fires** | H₂ fire can ignite aircraft materials | Monitor for secondary ignition |
| **Asphyxiation** | In confined spaces, combustion consumes O₂ | Ensure ventilation, SCBA required |

## 2. Immediate Response (First 2 Minutes)

### 2.1 Upon Discovery of H₂ Fire

**Step 1: ALERT** (10 seconds)
- Sound alarm: **"H₂ FIRE - EVACUATE"**
- Radio: "MAYDAY, MAYDAY, H₂ FIRE at [location]"
- Activate fire alarm pull station
- **IMMEDIATE ARFF dispatch**

**Step 2: EVACUATE** (90 seconds)
- Evacuate 50m minimum radius
- Move perpendicular to wind direction (avoid smoke/heat)
- Account for all personnel
- **NO RE-ENTRY** until ARFF clears

**Step 3: NOTIFY** (Simultaneous)
- ARFF (automatic dispatch)
- H₂ Safety Officer
- Airport operations
- Emergency medical services

## 3. H₂ Fire Response Strategy

### 3.1 Key Principle: **DO NOT EXTINGUISH**

**Why H₂ fires should not be extinguished:**
1. Extinguishing releases unburned H₂ into atmosphere
2. Unburned H₂ can accumulate and re-ignite explosively
3. Controlled burn is safer than uncontrolled release
4. H₂ fire will burn out when source depleted

**Exception:** May extinguish small H₂ fire if:
- Source can be immediately isolated
- No risk of H₂ accumulation
- ARFF commander approves
- Properly equipped personnel available

### 3.2 Firefighting Strategy

**Primary Actions:**
1. **Isolate H₂ Source** (if safe to do so)
   - Shut off H₂ flow remotely
   - Close upstream valves
   - Activate emergency shutoff systems

2. **Cool Surrounding Areas**
   - Apply water spray to aircraft structure
   - Prevent secondary fires
   - Protect exposures (other aircraft, buildings)
   - Maintain water spray until H₂ source depleted

3. **Let H₂ Burn**
   - Allow controlled combustion
   - Monitor fire progression
   - Ensure personnel safety perimeter maintained

## 4. Thermal Imaging for Fire Detection

### 4.1 Why Thermal Imaging Is Essential

H₂ flames are nearly invisible in daylight. Thermal imaging cameras detect:
- Flame location and size
- Thermal radiation intensity
- Hot surfaces
- Fire spread

### 4.2 Thermal Imaging Procedure

1. **Obtain thermal imaging camera** (operations center, ARFF)
2. **Scan from safe distance** (50m+)
3. **Identify flame** (bright white/yellow on thermal image)
4. **Map fire extent** (boundaries, hot spots)
5. **Monitor continuously** (track fire behavior)

---

## 5. Water Spray Application

### 5.1 Purpose of Water Spray

**NOT to extinguish H₂ fire** but to:
- Cool aircraft structure (prevent secondary fires)
- Absorb radiant heat
- Create thermal barrier
- Protect firefighters and equipment

### 5.2 Water Application Procedure

1. **Position fire apparatus** upwind, 30m+ from fire
2. **Deploy water spray nozzles** (wide pattern, not jet)
3. **Apply water to aircraft structure** around fire
   - Wings, fuselage, tail
   - NOT directly into H₂ flame (can disperse unburned H₂)
4. **Monitor runoff** (ensure drainage clear)
5. **Maintain spray** until fire out or source depleted

## 6. H₂ Source Isolation

### 6.1 Remote Isolation (Preferred)

**If available:**
- Activate aircraft emergency H₂ shutoff (remotely)
- Close H₂ tank isolation valves (remotely)
- Shut down H₂ ground supply (if fueling in progress)

### 6.2 Manual Isolation (Only If Safe)

**Attempt manual isolation ONLY if:**
- [ ] H₂ Safety Officer or ARFF Commander authorizes
- [ ] Trained personnel in full PPE + SCBA
- [ ] Thermal imaging confirms safe approach path
- [ ] Backup team ready

**Procedure:**
1. Approach from upwind with thermal imaging
2. Identify valve location
3. Close isolation valve quickly
4. Retreat immediately
5. Monitor for fire reduction

**DO NOT attempt if:**
- Direct fire exposure required
- Valve location unknown or inaccessible
- Inadequate PPE or thermal imaging

## 7. Evacuation and Safety Perimeters

### 7.1 Minimum Evacuation Distances

| Fire Size | Evacuation Radius | Rationale |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Small** (<1 m flame) | 30 m | Radiant heat |
| **Medium** (1-5 m flame) | 50 m | Radiant heat + potential tank rupture |
| **Large** (>5 m flame) | 100 m | Catastrophic tank failure potential |

### 7.2 Downwind Considerations

- **Water vapor plume**: Combustion product is steam (generally harmless)
- **Secondary combustion**: If other materials burning, evacuate downwind area

## 8. Aircraft H₂ Tank Fire

### 8.1 Tank Fire Scenarios

**LH₂ tank fire is most serious H₂ fire scenario:**
- Large volume of H₂ fuel involved
- Potential for tank rupture (BLEVE - Boiling Liquid Expanding Vapor Explosion)
- Extended burn time
- High thermal radiation

### 8.2 Tank Fire Response

1. **EVACUATE 100m minimum**
2. **ARFF only** — No ground crew operations
3. **DO NOT approach tank area**
4. **Establish water spray** at maximum safe distance
5. **Cool tank with water** (prevent rupture)
6. **Monitor continuously**
7. **Expect extended burn time** (hours possible)

### 8.3 BLEVE Risk

**If tank exposed to direct flame:**
- Tank walls heat up
- Internal pressure rises rapidly
- Risk of catastrophic rupture

**Indicators of imminent BLEVE:**
- Loud hissing or screaming sound from pressure relief
- Discoloration of tank surface
- Bulging or deformation of tank

**If BLEVE suspected:**
- **EVACUATE ALL PERSONNEL 200m+**
- **Abandon equipment**
- **Notify ARFF immediately**

## 9. Post-Fire Actions

### 9.1 Fire Out / Source Depleted

Declare **"FIRE OUT"** only when:
- [ ] No thermal signature on thermal imaging (sustained 5 minutes)
- [ ] No smoke or vapor
- [ ] H₂ detectors show <10% LEL
- [ ] Aircraft structure cooled (thermal imaging)
- [ ] ARFF commander approval

### 9.2 Aircraft Inspection

**Before approach:**
- [ ] ARFF declares safe
- [ ] H₂ concentrations <10% LEL
- [ ] Thermal imaging shows no hot spots
- [ ] Structural damage assessed from distance

**Initial inspection:**
- Approach with H₂ detector and thermal camera
- Check for residual H₂ (leaks)
- Assess fire damage
- Identify structural damage
- Document with photos

### 9.3 Investigation

1. **Preserve scene** (photography, measurements)
2. **Secure aircraft** (no unauthorized access)
3. **Notify authorities** (aviation, fire marshal, H₂ authority)
4. **Begin investigation** (within 24 hours)
5. **Root cause analysis**

## 10. Special Scenarios

### 10.1 H₂ Fire Inside Hangar

**Catastrophic scenario** — massive risk:
1. **EVACUATE BUILDING IMMEDIATELY**
2. **ARFF full response**
3. **DO NOT attempt interior firefighting** initially
4. **Open all doors/vents** (if safe)
5. **Consider letting building vent naturally**
6. **ARFF commander decision** on interior attack

### 10.2 H₂ Fire During Fueling

1. **STOP fueling immediately** (emergency shutoff)
2. **Disconnect fueling hose** (if safe — use quick-disconnect)
3. **EVACUATE fueling area**
4. **ARFF response**
5. **Follow H₂ fire procedures**
