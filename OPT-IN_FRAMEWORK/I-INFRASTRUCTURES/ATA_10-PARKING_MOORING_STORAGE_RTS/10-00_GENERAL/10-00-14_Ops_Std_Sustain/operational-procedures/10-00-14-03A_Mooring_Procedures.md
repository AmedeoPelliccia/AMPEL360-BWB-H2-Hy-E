## 1. Purpose

Procedures for mooring (tying down) the AMPEL360 BWB-H2-Hy-E aircraft to prevent movement or damage during high-wind conditions or extended parking.

---

## 2. Scope

- Tiedown operations for high-wind protection
- BWB-specific mooring patterns
- Tiedown equipment and anchors
- Mooring in various environmental conditions

---

## 3. When Mooring Is Required

Mooring (tiedown) is **mandatory** when:
- Forecast winds >35 kt sustained or >45 kt gusts
- Aircraft parked >7 days
- Severe weather expected (storms, hurricanes)
- Airport/facility requires mooring
- Aircraft parked in unsheltered location

Mooring is **recommended** when:
- Forecast winds >25 kt for extended period
- Aircraft parked at remote location
- Limited ground crew availability for monitoring

## 6. Mooring Procedure

### 6.1 Preparation

1. **Weather Briefing**: Review forecast winds and conditions
2. **Equipment Inspection**: Verify straps, anchors, tensioners serviceable
3. **Ground Anchor Check**: Inspect anchors secure and rated
4. **Personnel**: Minimum 4 personnel for mooring operations
5. **Aircraft Positioned**: Park aircraft in optimal orientation (nose into prevailing wind if known)

### 6.2 Tiedown Installation Sequence

**CRITICAL**: Follow this sequence to ensure balanced loads

1. **Nose Tiedowns** (2)
   - Attach straps to nose tiedown points
   - Route to ground anchors forward of aircraft
   - Apply initial tension (hand-tight)

2. **Inboard Wing Tiedowns** (4)
   - Attach to inboard wing points (left and right)
   - Route to ground anchors outboard and aft
   - Apply initial tension

3. **Outboard Wing Tiedowns** (4)
   - Attach to outboard wing/wingtip points
   - Route to ground anchors at maximum span
   - Apply initial tension

4. **Aft Tiedowns** (2)
   - Attach to aft body points
   - Route to ground anchors aft of aircraft
   - Apply initial tension

5. **Final Tensioning**
   - Using tensioners, bring all straps to target tension [TBD] kN
   - Tension evenly to avoid unbalanced loads
   - Verify load indicators show proper tension
   - Recheck after 1 hour and adjust as needed

### 6.3 Protective Padding

Install protective padding at all contact points to prevent chafing:
- Tiedown attachment points on aircraft
- Any point where strap contacts aircraft surface
- Use aviation-grade padding or purpose-built protectors

## 7. Mooring Inspection

### 7.1 Initial Inspection (Immediately After Installation)

- [ ] All 12 tiedown straps installed and tensioned
- [ ] Tension even across all straps
- [ ] No strap twisting or kinks
- [ ] Protective padding in place
- [ ] Ground anchors secure
- [ ] Wheel chocks in place
- [ ] Post "AIRCRAFT MOORED" placard

### 7.2 Periodic Inspection (During Mooring Period)

**Frequency**: Every 12 hours, or after winds >25 kt

- [ ] Strap tension: Retension if slack developed
- [ ] Strap condition: Check for fraying, chafing, damage
- [ ] Protective padding: Verify in place and effective
- [ ] Ground anchors: Ensure secure, no movement
- [ ] Aircraft position: Verify no movement from original position

---

## 8. Mooring Removal

### 8.1 When to Remove Mooring

Remove mooring when:
- Weather threat has passed
- Aircraft preparing for flight or towing
- Mooring is no longer required

### 8.2 Removal Procedure

**Sequence**: Reverse of installation

1. Release tension on all straps evenly
2. Remove aft tiedowns (2)
3. Remove outboard wing tiedowns (4)
4. Remove inboard wing tiedowns (4)
5. Remove nose tiedowns (2)
6. Inspect aircraft tiedown points for damage
7. Store equipment properly

---

## 9. Special Conditions

### 9.1 Hurricane/Typhoon Preparation

For extreme weather (winds >65 kt forecast):
- Consider relocating aircraft to sheltered location or hangar
- If mooring in place, use maximum tiedown configuration (all 12 points)
- Apply maximum tension [TBD] kN
- Inspect every 4 hours until storm passes
- Coordinate with airport emergency operations center

### 9.2 Snow/Ice Conditions

- Remove snow from wings before it accumulates (weight consideration)
- Check tiedown straps for ice accumulation
- Retension as needed (ice can cause slack)
- Use caution when removing mooring in freezing conditions (straps may be brittle)

### 9.3 H₂ Aircraft Mooring

**Additional Considerations for H₂-fueled aircraft:**
- Ensure H₂ safety zones maintained during mooring operations
- H₂ monitoring continues during mooring period
- Coordinate mooring with H₂ venting operations (if required)
- Emergency access must not be blocked by tiedown straps or equipment
