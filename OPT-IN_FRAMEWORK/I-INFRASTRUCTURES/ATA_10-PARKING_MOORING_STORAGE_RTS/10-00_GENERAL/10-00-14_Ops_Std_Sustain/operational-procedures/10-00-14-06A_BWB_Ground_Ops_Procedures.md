## 1. Purpose

This document provides procedures specific to ground operations of the Blended Wing Body (BWB) configuration of the AMPEL360 BWB-H2-Hy-E aircraft. The BWB's unique geometry requires special considerations for parking, towing, mooring, and ground equipment positioning.

## 3. BWB Parking Procedures

### 3.1 Parking Spot Requirements

**Minimum Clearances (all measurements from outermost point of aircraft):**
- **Wingtip to obstacle**: 7.5 m (25 ft) minimum
- **Nose to obstacle**: 5 m (16 ft) minimum
- **Tail to obstacle**: 5 m (16 ft) minimum
- **Adjacent aircraft**: 10 m (33 ft) wingtip to wingtip

**Surface Requirements:**
- Paved surface capable of supporting distributed BWB load
- Level within ±2° (pitch and roll)
- Good drainage (for H₂ operations)
- Access to ground power and H₂ servicing (if applicable)

### 3.2 BWB Parking Checklist

1. **Spot Preparation**
   - [ ] Verify parking spot dimensions adequate for BWB
   - [ ] Check surface condition and load capability
   - [ ] Confirm clearances to obstacles and adjacent aircraft
   - [ ] Position wing walkers at wingtips
   - [ ] Establish communication with tug operator

2. **Parking Approach**
   - [ ] Approach at slow speed (<5 km/h)
   - [ ] Wing walkers monitor clearances continuously
   - [ ] Stop aircraft on centerline markings
   - [ ] Verify longitudinal position correct
   - [ ] Set parking brake

3. **Final Positioning**
   - [ ] Verify aircraft aligned with parking spot markings
   - [ ] Check clearances all sides (visual confirmation)
   - [ ] Chock main gear (see section 3.3)
   - [ ] Install nose gear lock (if required)
   - [ ] Ground electrical bonding

### 3.3 BWB Wheel Chock Positioning

Due to BWB wide track main landing gear:

- **Main Gear**: Chock both main gear assemblies
  - Position chocks fore and aft of each main wheel
  - Use heavy-duty chocks rated for [TBD] kg per wheel
  - Secure chocks with tie-down straps or chains
  
- **Nose Gear**: Chock nose gear as per conventional practice
  - Standard chocks adequate

## 4. BWB Towing Procedures

### 4.1 Towing Considerations

The BWB's unique CG location and distributed weight require special towing procedures:

1. **Tow Bar Selection**: Use BWB-specific tow bar with longer reach
2. **Towing Speed**: Maximum 10 km/h (5 kt) — slower than conventional
3. **Turning Radius**: Wider turns required due to BWB geometry
4. **Wing Walkers**: Mandatory at both wingtips during all towing operations

### 4.2 Towing Procedure

1. **Pre-Tow Preparation**
   - [ ] Verify aircraft weight and CG within towing limits
   - [ ] Connect BWB-specific tow bar to nose gear
   - [ ] Install tow bar safety pins
   - [ ] Position wing walkers with radios
   - [ ] Establish communication (tug, aircraft, wing walkers)
   - [ ] Remove wheel chocks (main gear last)

2. **During Towing**
   - [ ] Towing speed ≤10 km/h
   - [ ] Wing walkers monitor clearances continuously
   - [ ] Wing walkers call out clearances every 10 m
   - [ ] Stop immediately if clearance <3 m to any obstacle
   - [ ] Make all turns slowly and smoothly
   - [ ] Monitor nose gear steering limits

3. **Post-Tow**
   - [ ] Position aircraft accurately
   - [ ] Set parking brake
   - [ ] Install wheel chocks
   - [ ] Disconnect tow bar
   - [ ] Verify aircraft secure

### 4.3 Emergency Tow Stop

If wing walker calls "STOP":
1. Tug operator immediately stops
2. Assess situation before proceeding
3. Re-plan tow route if necessary
4. Do not proceed until clearance confirmed safe

2. **Jacking Sequence**

## 8. BWB-Specific Safety Considerations

### 8.1 Clearance Management

- **Always use wing walkers** during aircraft movement
- **High-vis vests** required for all personnel near aircraft
- **Wingtip lighting** recommended for low-visibility conditions
- **Barriers** around parked aircraft in congested ramps

### 8.2 Personnel Movement

- **Under the aircraft**: Limited due to low ground clearance
- **Around wingtips**: Maintain 2 m clearance during operations
- **Approach paths**: Approach from nose or tail, not wingtips

### 8.3 FOD Prevention

BWB's wide body area and low clearance make it susceptible to FOD:
- Inspect ground area before parking
- Remove all FOD before aircraft arrival
- Continuous FOD watch during ground operations
