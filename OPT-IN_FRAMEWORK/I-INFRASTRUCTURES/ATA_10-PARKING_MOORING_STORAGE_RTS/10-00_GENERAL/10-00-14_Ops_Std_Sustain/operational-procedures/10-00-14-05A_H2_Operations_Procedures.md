## 1. Purpose

This document establishes mandatory procedures for all hydrogen (H₂) system operations during parking, mooring, storage, and return-to-service of the AMPEL360 BWB-H2-Hy-E aircraft. These procedures are **safety-critical** and must be followed without exception.

### 2.1 Applicability

- Pre-parking H₂ system checks
- H₂ monitoring during parking and storage
- H₂ venting procedures
- H₂ system reactivation for return-to-service
- Coordination with airport H₂ infrastructure

## 3. H₂ Hazard Overview

### 3.1 Key Hazards

| Hazard | Characteristics | Consequences | Mitigation |
|--------|----------------|--------------|------------|
| **H₂ Leak** | Wide flammability range (4-75% in air), low ignition energy | Explosion, fire | Continuous detection, ventilation |
| **H₂ Fire** | Nearly invisible flame, high radiant heat | Burns, property damage | Thermal imaging, water spray |
| **Asphyxiation** | H₂ displaces oxygen in confined spaces | Unconsciousness, death | Ventilation, O₂ monitoring |
| **Cryo Burns** | LH₂ at -253°C (-423°F) | Severe frostbite, tissue damage | Insulated PPE, training |
| **Embrittlement** | H₂ embrittles metals over time | Equipment failure | Material selection, inspection |

### 3.2 Lower Explosive Limit (LEL)

- **LEL**: 4% H₂ in air
- **Safety target**: Maintain <25% LEL in all areas
- **Evacuation trigger**: ≥25% LEL detected
- **Emergency response**: ≥50% LEL detected

## 4. H₂ Safety Zones

Refer to [10-00-14-42A_H2_Safety_Zones.md](../safety-operations/10-00-14-42A_H2_Safety_Zones.md) for detailed zone definitions.

### 4.1 Zone Summary

- **Hot Zone** (Red): 0-7.5 m from vent outlets and potential leak sources
  - No entry except certified H₂ handlers with full PPE
  - No ignition sources
  - Continuous H₂ monitoring required
  
- **Warm Zone** (Yellow): 7.5-15 m from hot zone perimeter
  - Limited access with H₂ handler supervision
  - No hot work permitted
  - H₂ monitoring required
  
- **Cold Zone** (Green): >15 m from hot zone perimeter
  - Standard operations permitted
  - Awareness of H₂ operations ongoing
  - Evacuation routes established

## 5. Pre-Parking H₂ System Checks

### 5.1 Pre-Check Requirements

**Prerequisites:**
- Aircraft on final approach or just landed
- H₂ system monitoring data available
- Parking spot prepared with H₂ safety zone established
- H₂ detection equipment operational and bump-tested

### 5.2 Pre-Parking Checklist

**Certified H₂ Handler Required**

1. **H₂ Tank Status Review**
   - [ ] Review in-flight H₂ system status logs
   - [ ] Check tank pressure: Normal range 0.5-5.0 bar (7-72 psi)
   - [ ] Verify tank temperature: -253°C ± 2°C
   - [ ] Review boil-off rate: Should be <1% per day
   - [ ] Check tank level: Document quantity remaining

2. **H₂ System Component Checks**
   - [ ] Vent system functional: Confirm vent valve operation
   - [ ] No H₂ leak alerts: Review aircraft health monitoring system
   - [ ] Isolation valves: Verify status and functionality
   - [ ] Pressure relief devices: Confirm normal operation
   - [ ] Ground connection points: Inspect for damage

3. **Environmental Assessment**
   - [ ] Wind direction: Identify downwind areas
   - [ ] Wind speed: <45 kt for parking operations
   - [ ] Temperature: Within operational limits (-20°C to +45°C)
   - [ ] Precipitation: No active precipitation during vent operations
   - [ ] Obstructions: Clear area around vent outlets

4. **Safety Equipment Verification**
   - [ ] H₂ detectors: Positioned and operational (4+ units)
   - [ ] Fire suppression: Water spray system ready
   - [ ] PPE: Cryo gloves, face shield, insulated clothing available
   - [ ] Communication: Radio check with operations center
   - [ ] Evacuation routes: Clear and marked

## 6. H₂ Venting Procedures During Parking

### 6.1 When Venting Is Required

H₂ venting during parking/storage is required when:
- Tank pressure exceeds 4.5 bar (65 psi)
- Boil-off rate exceeds 1.5% per day
- Extended parking >72 hours
- Storage period >7 days
- Tank maintenance required

### 6.2 Controlled Vent Procedure

**DANGER**: Only certified H₂ handlers may perform this procedure.

**Step-by-Step Procedure:**

1. **Pre-Vent Preparation** (15 minutes)
   - Establish H₂ safety zones (hot/warm/cold)
   - Position H₂ detectors at 0m, 5m, 10m, 15m from vent
   - Clear hot zone of all non-essential personnel
   - Verify wind direction and ventilation
   - Brief all personnel on vent operation
   - Alert airport operations and ARFF

2. **Connect Ground Vent (If Available)** (10 minutes)
   - Don full cryo PPE (insulated gloves, face shield, clothing)
   - Approach vent connection point from upwind
   - Inspect connection for frost or damage
   - Connect ground vent line (if airport equipped)
   - Verify secure connection and leak-free seal
   - Route vent to designated safe discharge area

3. **Initiate Controlled Vent** (Variable Duration)
   - Open vent valve slowly (0.5 turn per 5 seconds)
   - Monitor H₂ concentration continuously
   - Target vent rate: 5-10 kg/hour (adjust as needed)
   - Maintain hot zone integrity (no entry)
   - Monitor for ice formation on vent system
   - Record vent start time and initial pressure

4. **During Venting** (Continuous)
   - Monitor H₂ detectors: All readings must remain <25% LEL
   - Monitor wind direction: Adjust safety zones if wind shifts
   - Inspect vent plume (visible as cold vapor cloud)
   - Check for abnormal sounds, frost, or leaks
   - Log pressure, temperature, vent rate every 15 minutes
   - Maintain communication with operations center

5. **Terminate Vent** (10 minutes)
   - When target pressure reached (typically 1.5-2.0 bar)
   - Close vent valve slowly (0.5 turn per 5 seconds)
   - Allow system to stabilize (5 minutes)
   - Verify pressure stable and not rising rapidly
   - Disconnect ground vent (if used) with full PPE
   - Inspect connection point for frost or damage
   - Record final pressure, temperature, time

6. **Post-Vent Verification** (15 minutes)
   - Verify H₂ detectors show <10% LEL in all zones
   - Allow cold vapor to fully dissipate (wait 15 minutes minimum)
   - Inspect vent system for ice, frost, or damage
   - Document total H₂ vented (calculate from P-T data)
   - Update aircraft H₂ system status log
   - Release safety zones when H₂ clear

## 7. H₂ Monitoring During Storage

### 7.1 Monitoring Frequency

| Storage Duration | Monitoring Frequency | H₂ Detector Check | Actions |
|------------------|---------------------|-------------------|---------|
| **0-24 hours** | Every 4 hours | Every 8 hours | Visual inspection, data log |
| **1-7 days** | Every 8 hours | Every 12 hours | Pressure/temp trending |
| **7-30 days** | Every 12 hours | Daily | Vent if pressure >4.0 bar |
| **>30 days** | Daily | Daily | Active boil-off management |

### 7.2 Continuous Monitoring (Automated)

The aircraft H₂ system provides continuous monitoring of:
- Tank pressure and temperature
- H₂ leak detection
- Boil-off rate calculation
- Vent system status
- Isolation valve status

**Alerts are transmitted to:**
- Ground operations center
- H₂ Safety Officer (24/7 on-call)
- Airport operations

### 7.3 Manual Monitoring Procedure

**Performed by Certified H₂ Handler:**

1. **Approach Aircraft**
   - Review automated monitoring data first
   - Don appropriate PPE
   - Activate portable H₂ detector (bump test first)
   - Approach from upwind
   - Walk perimeter at 5m distance, checking for H₂ (if detector alarms, see emergency procedures)

2. **Visual Inspection**
   - Check vent outlets for ice/frost accumulation
   - Inspect tank area for visible damage or frost
   - Check ground area for pooling (should not occur with gaseous H₂, but indicates cryo leak if present)
   - Verify safety zone markings intact

3. **Data Recording**
   - Tank pressure (from aircraft monitoring system)
   - Tank temperature
   - Ambient conditions (temp, wind, humidity)
   - H₂ detector readings at multiple points
   - Any anomalies or concerns

4. **Trending Analysis**
   - Compare current data to previous readings
   - Calculate boil-off rate: ΔPressure / Δtime
   - Assess if venting will be required
   - Flag abnormal trends for H₂ Safety Officer review

## 8. H₂ System Reactivation (Return-to-Service)

### 8.1 Pre-Activation Checks

Before returning the aircraft to service:

1. **System Status Review**
   - [ ] Review entire storage monitoring log
   - [ ] Verify no H₂ leaks detected during storage
   - [ ] Check tank pressure and level suitable for flight
   - [ ] Confirm vent system functional
   - [ ] Verify isolation valves operational

2. **Component Inspection**
   - [ ] Visual inspection of H₂ tank area
   - [ ] Check all H₂ connections for damage or frost
   - [ ] Inspect vent outlets for blockage
   - [ ] Verify pressure relief devices not activated
   - [ ] Test H₂ leak detection system

3. **Safety Equipment Check**
   - [ ] H₂ detectors functional (bump test)
   - [ ] Fire suppression systems armed
   - [ ] PPE available for fueling operations
   - [ ] Emergency procedures briefed

### 8.2 Reactivation Procedure

**Certified H₂ Handler Required:**

1. **Pre-Flight H₂ System Power-Up**
   - Restore electrical power to H₂ monitoring systems
   - Allow sensor warm-up (5 minutes)
   - Verify all H₂ system indications normal
   - Check for any fault codes or alerts

2. **Functional Tests**
   - Test vent valve operation (open/close cycle)
   - Verify isolation valve operation
   - Check pressure and temperature sensor readings
   - Test H₂ leak detection system

3. **Final Verification**
   - Confirm tank pressure and level adequate for flight
   - Verify no active leaks
   - Check boil-off rate within normal limits
   - Document system status in aircraft logs

4. **Release to Operations**
   - Brief flight crew on H₂ system status during storage
   - Note any anomalies or concerns
   - Provide storage monitoring log summary
   - H₂ Safety Officer sign-off

## 9. Coordination with Airport H₂ Infrastructure

### 9.1 Ground Vent Connection

Many airports equipped with H₂ infrastructure provide:
- Ground vent connections (collect and route H₂ vent to safe area)
- H₂ monitoring systems
- H₂ firefighting equipment
- Trained H₂ response personnel

**Procedure for Using Ground Vent:**
1. Contact airport H₂ operations coordinator
2. Verify ground vent system available and functional
3. Request positioning of ground vent unit
4. Connect per airport-specific procedure (varies by airport)
5. Coordinate vent operations with airport ops
6. Disconnect and return equipment after vent complete

### 9.2 Airport H₂ Services

Coordinate with airport for:
- **H₂ fueling/defueling** (ATA 12 procedures, not covered here)
- **H₂ leak response** (airport ARFF may be first responder)
- **H₂ safety zone enforcement** (airport security)
- **H₂ incident investigation** (airport safety)

## 11. Abnormal Conditions

### 11.1 Rapid Pressure Rise

**Indication**: Tank pressure rising >0.5 bar/hour

**Probable Cause**: Excessive heat input or internal boil-off

**Action**:
1. Verify temperature sensors functional
2. Check for external heat sources (sun, equipment)
3. Consider immediate controlled vent
4. Notify H₂ Safety Officer
5. If pressure >5.5 bar, initiate emergency vent

### 11.2 H₂ Leak Detection

**Indication**: H₂ detector alarm (>25% LEL)

**Action**: Immediately execute [10-00-14-11A_H2_Leak_Emergency.md](../emergency-procedures/10-00-14-11A_H2_Leak_Emergency.md)

### 11.3 Vent System Failure

**Indication**: Vent valve will not open, or vent line blocked

**Action**:
1. Do not force vent valve
2. Monitor tank pressure continuously
3. If pressure rising toward relief valve setpoint (6.0 bar):
   - Evacuate personnel from hot zone
   - Allow pressure relief valve to vent (automatic)
   - Monitor for controlled relief
4. Contact H₂ system maintenance
5. Document incident

## 12. Documentation Requirements

### 12.1 Required Records

For each H₂ operation, document:
- Date, time, location
- Personnel (names, certifications)
- Aircraft registration
- H₂ system status (pressure, temperature, level)
- Actions taken (vent operations, monitoring, etc.)
- H₂ detector readings
- Environmental conditions
- Anomalies or incidents
- Sign-off by H₂ Safety Officer

### 12.2 Vent Operation Log

Specific vent log must include:
- Pre-vent pressure, temperature, level
- Vent start/end times
- Vent rate and total quantity vented
- H₂ detector readings (hot/warm/cold zones)
- Wind direction and speed
- Post-vent pressure, temperature
- Any alarms or abnormal conditions
