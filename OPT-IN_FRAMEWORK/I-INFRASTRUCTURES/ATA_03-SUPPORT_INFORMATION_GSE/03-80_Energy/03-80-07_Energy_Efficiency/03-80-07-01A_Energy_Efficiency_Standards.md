# 03-80-07-01A - Energy Efficiency Standards

## 1. Purpose
This document specifies energy efficiency standards and best practices for Ground Support Equipment (GSE) energy systems to minimize energy consumption and environmental impact.

## 2. Scope
This specification covers:
- Energy efficiency metrics and targets
- Equipment efficiency standards
- System efficiency optimization
- Performance benchmarking
- Continuous improvement programs

## 3. Applicable Documents
- ISO 50001 (Energy Management Systems)
- ISO 50006 (Measuring Energy Performance)
- ISO 50015 (Measurement and Verification of Energy Performance)
- Energy Star standards (where applicable)
- EPA SmartWay Program

## 4. Energy System Description

### 4.1 Overview
Energy efficiency standards establish minimum performance criteria and best practices to ensure GSE energy systems operate at optimal efficiency, reducing energy consumption, costs, and environmental impact.

### 4.2 Specifications
| Parameter | Specification | Notes |
|-----------|---------------|-------|
| Energy Efficiency Improvement Target | 30% by 2030 (baseline 2020) | Progressive targets |
| Equipment Efficiency | Top 25% of market | Procurement criteria |
| System Efficiency | >85% (grid to end-use) | Distribution and conversion losses |
| Energy Intensity Reduction | 2-3% per year | Continuous improvement |

### 4.3 Performance Requirements

**Energy Efficiency Metrics**:
- **Specific Energy Consumption (SEC)**: Energy per unit of output (kWh per vehicle serviced, per ton cargo moved, etc.)
- **Energy Intensity**: Energy per operational hour or per flight served
- **System Efficiency**: End-use energy / Primary energy input
- **Load Factor**: Average load / Peak load (higher is better)

**Benchmarking**:
- Compare performance against industry standards and peers
- Identify areas for improvement
- Track progress over time

### 4.4 Equipment Efficiency Standards

#### 4.4.1 Electric Motors
- Efficiency: IE3 (Premium Efficiency) or higher per IEC 60034-30-1
- Variable Frequency Drives (VFDs) for variable load applications

#### 4.4.2 Lighting
- LED lighting with >100 lm/W efficacy
- Occupancy sensors and daylight harvesting
- Smart lighting controls

#### 4.4.3 HVAC Systems
- High-efficiency chillers, boilers, heat pumps (SEER >14, COP >3)
- Variable air volume (VAV) systems
- Heat recovery ventilation

#### 4.4.4 Compressed Air Systems
- High-efficiency compressors
- Leak detection and repair program
- Pressure optimization

#### 4.4.5 Battery Charging Systems
- High-efficiency chargers (>90%)
- Smart charging to minimize losses
- Temperature-controlled charging

### 4.5 System Optimization

**Distribution Efficiency**:
- Minimize cable runs to reduce resistive losses
- Properly sized conductors
- Power factor correction (PF >0.95)

**Conversion Efficiency**:
- High-efficiency transformers (NEMA TP-1, or better)
- High-efficiency inverters for renewables and battery storage (>96%)
- Minimize conversion stages

**Operational Efficiency**:
- Right-sizing equipment to load (avoid oversized equipment running at low load)
- Regular maintenance to maintain peak efficiency
- Timely replacement of aging, inefficient equipment

### 4.6 Continuous Improvement

**Energy Audits**:
- Annual energy audits per ISO 50002
- Identify energy saving opportunities
- Prioritize based on cost-benefit analysis

**Energy Management System (EnMS)**:
- ISO 50001 certification
- Systematic approach to energy management
- Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle

**Training and Awareness**:
- Staff training on energy efficiency best practices
- Awareness campaigns to promote energy-saving behaviors
- Incentive programs for energy reduction achievements
