# 03-10-03-02A - Fueling Safety Protocols

## 4.4 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Requirements

### 4.4.1 Mandatory PPE for All Fueling Personnel

| PPE Item | Specification | Inspection Frequency |
|----------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Cryogenic Gloves** | ASTM F2249, liquid-proof, insulated to -253°C | Before each use |
| **Face Shield** | ANSI Z87.1+, full face, anti-fog, chin protection | Before each use |
| **Safety Glasses** | ANSI Z87.1, under face shield | Before each use |
| **Fire-Resistant Clothing** | NFPA 2112 or equivalent, covering all skin | Daily inspection |
| **Safety Shoes** | ASTM F2413, non-sparking, metatarsal protection | Weekly inspection |
| **Hard Hat** | ANSI Z89.1, Class E or G | Monthly inspection |
| **Personal H2 Detector** | 0-1000 ppm range, audible/visible alarm | Before each shift |

### 4.4.2 Optional/Conditional PPE

| PPE Item | When Required | Specification |
|----------|---------------|---------------|
| **Cryogenic Apron** | High splash risk operations | Liquid-proof, insulated |
| **Hearing Protection** | Near running engines or loud equipment | NRR 25 dB minimum |
| **Respiratory Protection** | Confined space or high H2 concentration | SCBA if >10% H2 |
| **Arc-Rated Clothing** | Electrical work near H2 | NFPA 70E compliant |

### 4.4.3 PPE Donning and Doffing Procedures

**Donning Sequence**:
1. Inspect all PPE for damage before donning
2. Don fire-resistant clothing (base layer)
3. Don hard hat
4. Don safety glasses
5. Don face shield
6. Don safety shoes (verify non-sparking)
7. Don cryogenic gloves (leave off until needed)
8. Activate personal H2 detector and verify alarm function

**Doffing Sequence**:
1. Move to designated PPE removal area
2. Remove cryogenic gloves (check for damage/contamination)
3. Remove face shield
4. Remove hard hat
5. Decontaminate if required
6. Store PPE properly for reuse or dispose as required

## 4.5 Safety Zones and Barriers

### 4.5.1 H2 Fueling Safety Zones

| Zone | Radius | Access Control | Activities Prohibited |
|------|--------|----------------|----------------------|
| **Primary Zone** | 15m (50 ft) | Essential personnel only with full PPE | Smoking, open flames, hot work, unauthorized equipment, mobile phones |
| **Secondary Zone** | 50m (165 ft) | Authorized personnel only | Smoking, open flames, non-essential vehicles |
| **Tertiary Zone** | 100m (330 ft) | Restricted access | Smoking within this zone |

### 4.5.2 Safety Barrier Requirements

- Physical barriers (cones, tape, fencing) at primary zone boundary
- Signage: "DANGER - HYDROGEN FUELING IN PROGRESS - NO SMOKING - NO IGNITION SOURCES"
- Warning lights or beacons during fueling operations
- Clear marking of emergency exits and assembly points
- Wind direction indicators at multiple points

## 4.6 H2 Detection and Monitoring

### 4.6.1 Detection Equipment Requirements

| Detector Type | Quantity | Location | Alarm Setpoint |
|---------------|----------|----------|----------------|
| **Fixed H2 Detector** | Minimum 2 | Aircraft fuel panel area, fuel truck/hydrant | 25% LEL (1% H2) |
| **Portable H2 Detector** | Minimum 2 | Carried by fueling personnel | 25% LEL (1% H2) |
| **Personal H2 Detector** | 1 per person | Worn by all fueling personnel | 10% LEL (0.4% H2) |

### 4.6.2 Detection System Calibration

- Calibrate all H2 detectors before each shift
- Use certified calibration gas (known H2 concentration)
- Document calibration in equipment log
- Replace sensors per manufacturer schedule
- Bump test all detectors at start of each fueling operation

### 4.6.3 Alarm Response Procedures

**Low Level Alarm (10-25% LEL)**:
1. Announce "H2 Detected - Caution"
2. Increase monitoring frequency
3. Check for source with portable detector
4. Increase ventilation if possible
5. Prepare to stop fueling if concentration increases

**High Level Alarm (>25% LEL or 1% H2)**:
1. **STOP FUELING IMMEDIATELY**
2. Announce "H2 LEAK - EVACUATE"
3. Activate emergency shutdown
4. Evacuate to upwind assembly point
5. Account for all personnel
6. Notify emergency services
7. Do not re-enter until H2 concentration <10% LEL and source identified

### 4.7.2 Emergency Communication

- Designate primary and backup emergency coordinators
- Test communication equipment before operations
- Establish clear emergency communication protocol
- Pre-program emergency numbers in all radios
- Maintain contact with airport emergency services

### 4.7.3 Emergency Assembly Points

- Establish primary and alternate assembly points upwind
- Minimum 100m (330 ft) from fueling location
- Clearly marked and known to all personnel
- Protected from weather
- Equipped with first aid and communication

### 4.7.4 Emergency Response Team Coordination

Before each fueling operation:
1. Notify airport emergency services of fueling schedule
2. Provide location and estimated duration
3. Confirm emergency services available and ready
4. Brief emergency services on H2-specific hazards
5. Establish direct communication link
6. Conduct joint drills quarterly

## 4.8 Weather Limitations

### 4.8.1 Prohibited Conditions

**DO NOT CONDUCT H2 FUELING** when:

| Condition | Limit | Reason |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| **Lightning** | Within 5 miles (8 km) | Ignition risk |
| **Wind Speed** | >40 knots sustained | Equipment control, dispersion |
| **Visibility** | <1/4 mile (400m) | Safety monitoring |
| **Precipitation** | Heavy rain, snow, or ice accumulation | Equipment function, safety |
| **Temperature** | Outside equipment rating (-40°C to +50°C typically) | Equipment reliability |
| **Tornado/Severe Storm** | Any active warning | Personnel safety |

### 4.8.2 Weather Monitoring Requirements

- Monitor weather continuously during fueling
- Check forecast before starting operation
- Establish weather decision authority
- Implement 30-minute wait after lightning
- Stop immediately if conditions deteriorate

## 5. Safety Considerations

### 5.1 H2 Hazard Summary

**DANGER - HYDROGEN**:
- Extremely flammable (4-75% in air)
- Odorless, colorless, tasteless (undetectable without instruments)
- Low ignition energy (0.02 mJ - 1/10 of gasoline)
- Wide explosive range
- Burns with nearly invisible flame
- Lighter than air (rises and disperses rapidly)
- Can accumulate in confined spaces or under overhangs
- Extreme cold (liquid at -253°C / -423°F)

### 5.2 Critical Safety Rules

**ZERO TOLERANCE RULES** (immediate removal from operation):
1. Smoking or bringing smoking materials into secondary zone
2. Using unauthorized ignition sources
3. Operating non-approved electronic devices in primary zone
4. Working without required PPE
5. Bypassing safety systems or procedures
6. Working under the influence of alcohol or drugs
7. Falsifying records or certifications
8. Failure to respond to H2 detection alarm

## 6. Emergency Actions

### 6.1 LH2 Exposure (Cold Injury)

**IMMEDIATE ACTIONS**:
1. Move victim away from hazard
2. Do NOT rub affected area
3. Remove any clothing stuck to skin by cutting around it (do not pull)
4. Immerse affected area in warm (NOT hot) water (37-39°C / 99-102°F)
5. Cover with sterile, non-adhesive dressing
6. Seek immediate medical attention
7. Monitor for shock

### 6.2 H2 Inhalation (Asphyxiation)

**IMMEDIATE ACTIONS**:
1. Move victim to fresh air immediately
2. Loosen tight clothing
3. If not breathing, begin rescue breathing
4. If no pulse, begin CPR
5. Administer oxygen if available and trained
6. Keep victim warm
7. Seek immediate medical attention
8. Do NOT enter high H2 concentration area without SCBA

### 6.3 H2 Fire

**See Section 6.2 of 03-10-03-01A for detailed procedures**

Key points:
- Evacuate area
- Do not attempt to extinguish unless specifically trained
- Allow to burn out if safe
- Cool surrounding structures
- Call professional fire brigade
