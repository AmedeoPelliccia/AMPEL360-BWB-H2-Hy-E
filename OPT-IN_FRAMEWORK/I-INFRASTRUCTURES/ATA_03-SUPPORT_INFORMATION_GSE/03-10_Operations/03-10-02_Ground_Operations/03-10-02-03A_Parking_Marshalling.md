# 03-10-02-03A - Parking and Marshalling

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes standardized parking and marshalling procedures for the AMPEL360 BWB-H2-Hy-E aircraft, ensuring safe aircraft positioning at gates, parking stands, and remote locations.

## 2. Scope

This procedure covers:
- Marshalling signals and procedures
- Visual guidance systems
- Aircraft parking alignment and positioning
- H2 safety zone considerations
- Special considerations for BWB aircraft configuration
- Ground crew positioning and safety

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Prerequisites

- Marshaller trained and qualified for aircraft type
- Gate/parking position prepared and clear
- Visual guidance system operational (if equipped)
- Communication equipment operational
- H2 safety zones marked and understood
- Weather conditions acceptable for marshalling
- Adjacent aircraft positions considered

### 4.2 Equipment Required

- Marshalling wands (illuminated for night operations)
- High-visibility vest with reflective markings
- Hearing protection
- Communication headset (if available)
- Chocks (minimum 2 sets for main gear)
- Parking position markers/guidance system
- H2 hazard warning signs
- Fire extinguisher (nearby)

## 5. Safety Considerations

### 5.1 BWB Aircraft Specific Considerations

**CAUTION**:
- BWB configuration has wider wingspan than conventional aircraft
- Wingtip clearances require extra attention
- Center of aircraft may appear closer than wings indicate
- Marshaller positioning critical for pilot visibility
- Consider using wingwalkers for tight parking situations

### 5.2 H2 Safety Zones

**WARNING**:
- Maintain minimum 3-meter safety zone around H2 vent areas
- No ignition sources within H2 hazard zones
- Post H2 warning signs immediately after parking
- Ensure ground crew aware of H2 system locations
- Coordinate with H2 fueling teams before approach

### 5.3 Marshalling Safety

**WARNING**:
- Never position yourself in propeller or jet intake/exhaust areas
- Maintain awareness of other aircraft and ground vehicles
- Stop marshalling immediately if unsafe condition develops
- Ensure marshaller clearly visible to flight crew at all times
- Use additional personnel (wingwalkers) when clearances tight

**CAUTION**:
- Wet or icy ramps require extra caution
- High winds affect aircraft handling during parking
- Night operations require adequate lighting
- Ensure marshaller not distracted during critical phases

### 5.4 Standard Marshalling Signals

Follow ICAO Annex 14 standard marshalling signals:
- Arms extended, palms facing outward = "Straight Ahead"
- Arms crossed above head = "Stop" / "Brakes"
- Arms moving up and down = "Speed Up"
- Arms moving slowly = "Slow Down"
- Left arm down, right arm pointing = "Turn Right"
- Right arm down, left arm pointing = "Turn Left"
- Arms raised, palms inward = "Chocks Inserted"

## 6. Emergency Actions

### 6.1 Marshalling Emergency Stop

If immediate stop required during marshalling:
1. Signal "STOP" immediately (cross arms above head)
2. Hold stop signal until aircraft completely stopped
3. Assess the hazard
4. Communicate issue to cockpit via radio
5. Do not proceed until hazard resolved
6. Consider alternate parking location if necessary

### 6.2 H2 Leak During Parking

If H2 leak detected or suspected during parking:
1. Signal "STOP" immediately
2. Keep aircraft at safe distance from buildings/other aircraft
3. Notify cockpit of H2 leak via radio
4. Request H2 emergency response team
5. Evacuate non-essential personnel
6. Establish safety perimeter
7. Do not allow aircraft to final parking position
8. Refer to 03-10-07-02A H2 Emergency Response

### 6.3 Aircraft Clearance Issue

If wingtip or fuselage clearance issue identified:
1. Signal "STOP" immediately
2. Assess clearance using wingwalkers
3. Communicate with cockpit
4. Guide aircraft backward if safe and necessary
5. Reposition to alternate parking spot if required
6. Never compromise safety clearances

### 6.4 Marshaller Incapacitation

If marshaller becomes incapacitated:
1. Backup marshaller immediately signals "STOP"
2. Aircraft holds position with brakes set
3. New marshaller assumes position
4. Brief cockpit on situation via radio
5. Resume marshalling when safe
6. Complete incident report after aircraft secured
