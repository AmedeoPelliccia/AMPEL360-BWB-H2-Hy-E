# 03-10-02-04A - Towing and Pushback

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes standardized towing and pushback procedures for the AMPEL360 BWB-H2-Hy-E aircraft, ensuring safe ground movement operations with appropriate considerations for the BWB configuration and H2 fuel systems.

## 2. Scope

This procedure covers:
- Pushback operations from gate/parking
- Towing operations for maintenance or repositioning
- Tug attachment and detachment procedures
- H2 system safety during towing operations
- Communication procedures between cockpit and ground crew
- Special considerations for BWB aircraft

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Prerequisites

**For Pushback**:
- Aircraft properly secured at gate
- Flight crew on board and ready
- All doors closed except as required
- Ground power connected or APU running
- Tug rated for aircraft weight
- Tug driver qualified for aircraft type
- Clear pushback route confirmed

**For Towing**:
- Aircraft prepared for towing per AMM
- Towing clearance obtained
- Qualified towing supervisor present
- Towing route surveyed and clear
- H2 system in safe configuration

### 4.2 Equipment Required

- Pushback tug or towbar tug (appropriate capacity)
- Tow bar (if applicable)
- Communication equipment (intercom or radio)
- Wheel chocks
- Bypass pins (if required for towing)
- Towing lights (for visibility)
- Fire extinguisher nearby
- H2 leak detection equipment

## 5. Safety Considerations

### 5.1 BWB Aircraft Towing Considerations

**CAUTION**:
- BWB configuration has different weight distribution than conventional aircraft
- Wide wingspan requires extra clearance monitoring
- Center of gravity considerations affect towing dynamics
- Tail clearance less critical but wing clearance more critical
- Use wing walkers for any tight clearance situations
- Tug capacity must account for BWB weight and configuration

### 5.2 H2 Fuel System Safety During Towing

**WARNING**:
- Verify H2 system in safe configuration before towing
- No H2 fueling or defueling operations during tow
- Monitor for H2 leaks throughout towing operation
- Keep ignition sources away from H2 vent areas
- Emergency H2 shutdown procedures must be immediately available
- Towing supervisor must be H2-safety qualified

**CAUTION**:
- H2 tank venting may occur during temperature changes
- Rough towing can affect H2 system integrity
- Ensure H2 emergency response team aware of towing operations

### 5.3 General Towing Safety

**WARNING**:
- Never exceed tug capacity rating
- Maintain positive communication at all times
- Stop immediately if any safety concern arises
- Never tow in high wind conditions exceeding limits
- Ensure all personnel clear of gear, doors, and control surfaces

**CAUTION**:
- Maintain walking pace speed during towing
- Make wide turns to prevent gear damage
- Avoid sudden starts or stops
- Monitor brake temperature if towing long distances
- Check tow bar and connections regularly during long tows

### 5.4 Communication Protocols

- Establish clear hand signals before operations
- Use standard ICAO phraseology on radio
- Confirm all critical commands (brake release, brake set)
- Maintain constant situational awareness
- Brief "stop" procedures to all crew members

## 6. Emergency Actions

### 6.1 H2 Leak During Towing

If H2 leak detected during pushback or towing:
1. Stop movement immediately
2. Set parking brake (if cockpit occupied)
3. Install wheel chocks
4. Evacuate non-essential personnel
5. Notify H2 emergency response team
6. Establish safety perimeter
7. Do not continue towing operation
8. Refer to 03-10-07-02A H2 Emergency Response

### 6.2 Tow Bar/Connection Failure

If tow bar breaks or connection fails:
1. Stop tug immediately
2. Set aircraft parking brake if possible
3. Install wheel chocks
4. Assess damage and safety situation
5. Do not attempt to reconnect damaged equipment
6. Request alternate tug and equipment
7. Inspect aircraft for damage before proceeding

### 6.3 Loss of Communication

If communication lost between cockpit and tug:
1. Stop towing/pushback immediately
2. Set parking brake and install chocks
3. Re-establish communication via backup method
4. Do not proceed until communication restored
5. Brief reason for delay and restart procedures
6. Consider aborting operation if communication unreliable

### 6.4 Aircraft Clearance Issue

If obstacle or clearance issue identified:
1. Stop movement immediately
2. Assess clearances from safe position
3. Reposition wing walkers if needed
4. Determine if safe to proceed
5. Consider alternate route if necessary
6. Never compromise minimum clearances

### 6.5 Tug Failure or Breakdown

If tug fails during operation:
1. Set aircraft parking brake
2. Install wheel chocks immediately
3. Secure tow bar connection
4. Request backup tug
5. Brief new tug driver on situation
6. Transfer towing operation safely to new tug
