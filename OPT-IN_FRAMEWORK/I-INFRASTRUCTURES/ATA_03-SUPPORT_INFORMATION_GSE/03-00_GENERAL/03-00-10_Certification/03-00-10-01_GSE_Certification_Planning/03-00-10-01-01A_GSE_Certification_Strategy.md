# 03-00-10-01-01A - GSE Certification Strategy

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the **certification strategy** for Ground Support Equipment (GSE) supporting the AMPEL360 BWB H₂ Hy-E aircraft. It defines the overall approach, principles, governance, and stakeholder engagement framework for ensuring that all GSE meets applicable regulatory requirements, safety standards, and operational suitability criteria.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 Coverage

This certification strategy applies to:

- **Hydrogen Fuel GSE**: LH₂ fueling systems, cryogenic handling equipment, safety systems
- **Electrical GSE**: Ground power units, battery chargers, electrical test equipment
- **Mechanical GSE**: Towing tractors, maintenance platforms, cargo loaders
- **Environmental GSE**: Air conditioning units, heating systems, water/waste servicing
- **Digital GSE**: Diagnostic systems, data loaders, software update equipment
- **Safety GSE**: Fire suppression systems, emergency response equipment, personnel protection

### 2.2 Out of Scope

- Aircraft systems certification (covered under aircraft certification basis)
- Airport infrastructure (covered under airport operations approval)
- Third-party commercial GSE not specific to AMPEL360 aircraft

## 4. Certification Requirements

### 4.1 Overview

GSE certification for AMPEL360 follows a **three-tier approach**:

1. **Type Certification**: Design approval for GSE types
2. **Operational Approval**: Suitability for specific operational contexts
3. **Continued Airworthiness**: Ongoing compliance and safety monitoring

### 4.2 Regulatory Basis

The certification strategy is founded on:

- **Aircraft interface compliance**: Ensuring GSE interfaces correctly with aircraft systems without compromising airworthiness
- **Operational safety**: Demonstrating safe operation in airport environments
- **Environmental compliance**: Meeting emissions, noise, and sustainability requirements
- **Personnel safety**: Protecting ground crew and maintenance personnel

### 4.3 Compliance Methods

| Requirement Type | Compliance Method | Evidence |
|------------------|-------------------|----------|
| Design compliance | Analysis, testing, similarity | Design reports, test data, comparative analysis |
| Operational suitability | Operational trials, simulation | Trial reports, operator feedback, risk assessments |
| Safety compliance | Hazard analysis, FHA, FMEA | Safety case, hazard logs, mitigation verification |
| Interface compliance | Interface verification, compatibility testing | ICD verification, interface test reports |
| Regulatory compliance | Compliance matrix, regulatory review | Compliance declarations, authority approvals |

## 6. Safety Requirements

### 6.1 General Safety Principles

All GSE must:

- Incorporate fail-safe design principles
- Prevent damage to aircraft systems
- Protect personnel from hazards
- Include emergency stop and shutdown systems
- Comply with ATEX requirements for explosive atmospheres (H₂ zones)

### 6.2 Hydrogen-Specific Safety

For H₂ GSE:

- Double-wall cryogenic containment (-253°C)
- Leak detection and automatic shutdown
- Explosion-proof electrical systems (Zone 1/2)
- Personnel safety interlocks
- Emergency response compatibility

### 6.3 Safety Assurance Process

- Preliminary hazard analysis during design
- System hazard analysis during development
- Operating hazard analysis for operational scenarios
- Fault tree analysis for critical systems
- Continuous safety monitoring in service

## 8. Governance and Stakeholder Engagement

### 8.1 Certification Organization

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|----------------|
| **Chief Engineer, GSE** | Overall certification strategy and approval |
| **Certification Manager** | Coordination and regulatory liaison |
| **Design Authority** | Design compliance and evidence |
| **Safety Manager** | Safety case and hazard management |
| **Quality Manager** | Quality assurance and audit |
| **Test Manager** | Test planning and execution |

### 8.2 External Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Engagement Approach |
|-------------|---------------------|
| **EASA / NAAs** | Formal certification applications, technical meetings, issue papers |
| **FAA** | Bilateral agreement coordination, technical data submission |
| **Airport Authorities** | Operational suitability demonstrations, acceptance trials |
| **GSE Manufacturers** | Design collaboration, quality oversight, supplier audits |
| **Ground Service Providers** | Operational feedback, training coordination, procedure validation |

### 8.3 Certification Review Board (CRB)

A **Certification Review Board** is established to:

- Review and approve certification plans
- Monitor certification progress
- Resolve technical issues
- Approve compliance declarations
- Authorize certification dossier submission

**Composition**: Chief Engineer GSE (Chair), Certification Manager, Safety Manager, Design Authority representatives, Quality Manager, external advisors as needed.

**Meetings**: Monthly during active certification phases, quarterly for continued airworthiness monitoring.

## 9. Certification Phases

### 9.1 Phase 1: Planning and Requirements (Months 1-6)

- Define certification basis
- Establish regulatory requirements
- Develop certification plans
- Identify means of compliance
- Engage with authorities

### 9.2 Phase 2: Design and Development (Months 7-18)

- GSE detailed design
- Safety assessments
- Preliminary testing
- Design reviews
- Supplier qualification

### 9.3 Phase 3: Verification and Testing (Months 19-30)

### 9.4 Phase 4: Certification Approval (Months 31-36)

- Compliance dossier preparation
- Authority review
- Issue resolution
- Certification issuance

### 9.5 Phase 5: Continued Airworthiness (Ongoing)

- In-service monitoring
- Incident investigation
- Modification approval
- Recertification as required
- Regulatory surveillance

## 10. Risk Management

### 10.1 Certification Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|-----------|
| Novel H₂ GSE technology | Delays, special conditions | Early engagement with authorities, prototype demonstrations |
| Evolving H₂ regulations | Scope changes, rework | Regulatory monitoring, flexible design approach |
| Supplier capacity | Delays, quality issues | Dual sourcing, quality oversight, early supplier engagement |
| Operational acceptance | Limited airport adoption | Early trials, operator engagement, training support |
| Interface incompatibilities | Re-design, delays | Early interface verification, mockups, compatibility testing |

### 10.2 Mitigation Strategies

- **Early authority engagement**: Begin discussions with EASA/FAA in Phase 1
- **Incremental certification**: Certify conventional GSE first, then novel H₂ systems
- **Prototype validation**: Build and test prototypes before full-scale production
- **Regulatory monitoring**: Track evolving H₂ standards and regulations
- **Risk reviews**: Monthly risk reviews by CRB

## 11. Quality Assurance

### 11.1 Quality Management

All GSE certification activities are conducted under **ISO 9001** quality management system:

- Design reviews and audits
- Supplier quality oversight
- Test procedure approval
- Evidence verification
- Document control and configuration management

### 11.2 Audit and Surveillance

- Internal audits of certification processes (quarterly)
- Supplier audits (annually, or pre-production)
- Authority surveillance (as required)
- Post-certification audits (annually for continued airworthiness)
