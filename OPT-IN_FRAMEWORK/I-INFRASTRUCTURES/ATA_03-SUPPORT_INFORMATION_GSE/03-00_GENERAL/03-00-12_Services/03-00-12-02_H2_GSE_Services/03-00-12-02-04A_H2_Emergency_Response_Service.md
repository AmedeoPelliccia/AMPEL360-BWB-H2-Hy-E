# 03-00-12-02-04A - H2 Emergency Response Service

## 1. Purpose
This document defines the emergency response services for hydrogen-related incidents during AMPEL360 aircraft ground operations, including procedures, resources, and coordination protocols.

## 2. Scope
This service specification covers:
- H2 emergency response team composition and readiness
- Emergency response procedures and protocols
- Equipment and resource requirements
- Coordination with airport emergency services
- Post-incident investigation and reporting

## 4. Service Description

### 4.1 Overview
H2 Emergency Response Service provides immediate, specialized response capability for any hydrogen-related emergency during ground operations. The service maintains trained personnel, specialized equipment, and established procedures to mitigate hydrogen incidents and protect personnel, aircraft, and facilities.

### 4.2 Service Specifications
| Parameter | Specification | SLA Target |
|-----------|---------------|------------|
| Team Availability | 24/7/365 | Continuous on-site presence during H2 ops |
| Response Time | ≤ 2 minutes | From alert to team deployment |
| Team Size | Minimum 4 personnel | Per active H2 operation area |
| Equipment Readiness | 100% | All emergency equipment operational |
| Communication Redundancy | Triple redundancy | Radio, cellular, hardline |
| Emergency Drill Frequency | Weekly | Tabletop and full-scale drills |
| Coordination with Airport Fire | <3 minutes | Notification and coordination time |

### 4.3 Service Delivery Process
1. **Readiness Phase**: Pre-positioning of emergency response team and equipment
2. **Detection Phase**: Alert received from safety monitoring system or personnel
3. **Activation Phase**: Emergency response team deploys to incident location
4. **Assessment Phase**: Incident commander evaluates situation and determines response level
5. **Response Phase**: Execute appropriate emergency procedures (evacuation, suppression, containment)
6. **Stabilization Phase**: Secure area and prevent escalation
7. **Recovery Phase**: Safe return to normal operations after clearance
8. **Investigation Phase**: Root cause analysis and corrective action planning

## 6. Safety Requirements

### 6.1 Emergency Response Levels
**Level 1 - Minor Incident**
- Small H2 leak or equipment malfunction
- No immediate danger to personnel or aircraft
- Response: Local team handles with established procedures
- Notification: Safety manager and shift supervisor

**Level 2 - Moderate Incident**
- Significant H2 leak or cryogenic release
- Potential for escalation
- Response: Full emergency team deployment, airport fire notification
- Notification: Airport operations, emergency services, AMPEL360 management

**Level 3 - Major Incident**
- Large H2 release, fire, or explosion
- Immediate danger to personnel and/or aircraft
- Response: Full emergency response, airport-wide coordination, evacuation
- Notification: All emergency services, regulatory authorities, executive management

### 6.2 Required Emergency Equipment
- H2-rated fire suppression systems (water deluge, foam)
- Specialized H2 leak detection equipment (portable)
- Cryogenic spill containment materials
- Emergency shut-off controls (portable backup systems)
- Personal protective equipment (SCBA, cryogenic suits)
- Emergency evacuation equipment
- Communication systems (redundant)
- First aid and medical response equipment

### 6.3 Personnel Requirements
- Minimum 4 certified H2 emergency responders on-site during all H2 operations
- Annual H2 emergency response training and certification
- Quarterly refresher training and drills
- Medical clearance for SCBA use
- Proficiency in H2 properties, hazards, and emergency procedures
- Coordination training with airport fire services
